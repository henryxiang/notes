# How to Reduce A1C

A1C reflects your average blood sugar over the past ~3 months. Lowering it requires consistent effort across several areas.

## Diet (biggest lever)
- Reduce refined carbs and added sugars (soda, juice, sweets, white bread/rice)
- Prioritize non-starchy vegetables, lean protein, healthy fats, and fiber
- Consider carb counting or the plate method (½ veg, ¼ protein, ¼ carbs)
- Watch portion sizes and meal timing

## Physical activity
- Aim for ~150 min/week of moderate exercise (brisk walking, cycling)
- Add resistance training 2–3×/week — muscle improves insulin sensitivity
- Even short walks after meals help blunt blood sugar spikes

## Medication (if prescribed)
- Take metformin or other diabetes meds as directed
- Some people need insulin or GLP-1 receptor agonists — follow your doctor's plan

## Weight management
- Losing even 5–10% of body weight can meaningfully lower A1C

## Other habits
- Sleep 7–9 hours — poor sleep raises insulin resistance
- Manage stress (cortisol raises blood sugar)
- Stay hydrated; limit alcohol

## Monitoring
- Check blood sugar regularly to see what works for you
- Retest A1C every 3 months to track progress

## Realistic expectations
A1C changes slowly. A drop of 0.5–1% over 3 months is a solid, achievable goal with consistent effort.

---

**Note:** This is general health information, not medical advice. If you have diabetes or prediabetes, work with your doctor or a registered dietitian to set a target and plan that's right for you — especially before changing medications or starting an intense exercise program.
