## 2. What is driving the open

### Nvidia: a beat and raise, but a smaller premarket gain

- **Price.** Nvidia is up about 3.1% near $160.20 at 8:40 AM ET, against Tuesday's $155.46 close. It was near $162.4 at about 7:53 PM ET Tuesday, and Yahoo's premarket high is $161.68. Monday's high was $163.20.
- **Results recap.** Fiscal third-quarter revenue was $115.84 billion against $109.10 billion expected, and fourth-quarter guidance was $130 billion, plus or minus 3%, against about $125.25 billion (LSEG via Reuters). The guidance excludes Data Center compute revenue from China ([Nvidia](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx)). Details are in section 8 of the [November 17 note](2026-11-17-market-watch.md).
- **SEC report.** The Wall Street Journal reported Tuesday that the SEC is opening a civil probe into whether Nvidia's disclosures misled investors ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/sec-reportedly-investigating-nvidia-amid-huang-exit/)). Nvidia had not commented in the reports checked, and the probe has not been confirmed by the SEC or the company.
- **Leadership.** Nvidia Chair Harvey Jones said on the earnings call that the board does not intend to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO ([Reuters via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/nvidia-board-says-does-not-221236394.html)). Huang is scheduled to testify before lawmakers in December on national security and export controls (Investing.com via Yahoo).
- **Our interpretation:** Nvidia has given back about a third of its after-hours gain. The numbers were strong, but the overhang now is governance and regulation, not demand. A gap that holds above $158.19 (Tuesday's high on CNBC) would show buyers accepting the results; a fade toward $155 would mean the good news is being sold.

### Retailers: strong sales, but tariff refunds cloud the profits

- **Target.** Third-quarter net sales rose 7.1% to $27.07 billion, against $26.56 billion expected (LSEG via [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/target-tgt-q3-2026-earnings.html)). Comparable sales rose 5.6% against 3.8% expected (StreetAccount), with traffic up 5.0%, store comps up 4.6% and digital up 9.8%. EPS was $2.65, including a $0.50 benefit from tariff refunds; excluding the refunds, adjusted EPS rose 21%. Target raised its full-year sales growth outlook to about 6% and its EPS range to $10.65 to $11.40, from $9.90 to $10.90, and said November sales to date are accelerating from October ([Target 8-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000027419/000002741926000051/a2026q3ex-99.htm)). The stock is down about 0.5%, after trading between about $153.40 and $163.00 premarket.
- **Lowe's.** Sales rose 9.3% to $22.76 billion, and comparable sales rose 1.8% against an estimated 0.4% (Reuters via [Investing.com](https://investing.com/news/stock-market-news/lowes-raises-annual-sales-forecast-5035489)). Adjusted EPS was $3.10, including an estimated $0.34 benefit from tariff refunds, against $2.88 to $2.89 expected. Lowe's raised its full-year sales outlook to about $92.5 billion and its comparable-sales outlook to about +0.5%, from flat. It trimmed its operating-margin outlook to about 11.1% from 11.2% and reaffirmed adjusted EPS of about $12.25, against a $12.29 consensus ([Lowe's](https://corporate.lowes.com/newsroom/press-releases/lowes-reports-third-quarter-2026-sales-and-earnings-results), [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/lowes-beats-third-quarter-estimates-on-strong-sales-growth-5035490)). The stock is down about 5.7% near $208, and Home Depot is down about 1.8%.
- **TJX.** Net sales rose 6% to $15.95 billion, and comparable sales rose 4%, above its 2% to 3% plan. EPS was $1.39, or $1.34 excluding a $0.05 net benefit from tariff refunds and related compensation accruals. TJX now expects full-year comparable sales growth of 4%, GAAP EPS of $5.33 to $5.36 and adjusted EPS of $5.17 to $5.20 ([TJX 8-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/109198/000010919826000058/tjxq3fy27earningspressrele.htm)). The stock is up about 5.7%.
- **Our interpretation:** The three reports show consumers still spending, especially at discount and value-oriented chains, which fits Tuesday's firm retail-sales control group. The market is separating clean beats (TJX) from beats that rely on tariff refunds and margins under pressure (Lowe's). Lowe's margin trim, Home Depot's decline and weak building permits make home improvement the weakest part of the consumer picture. Walmart reports Thursday.

### Housing starts: a strong headline, weak permits

- **Starts.** October housing starts rose 9.9% to a 1,309,000 annual rate, from a revised 1,191,000 in September, against a 4.4% forecast. The Census Bureau's 90% confidence interval of ±16.8% includes zero. Single-family starts rose 4.0% to 864,000 ([Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/construction/nrc/pdf/newresconst.pdf)).
- **Permits.** Building permits fell 7.4% to 1,295,000, from a revised 1,398,000, against a forecast decline of 1.6%, and were 8.7% below a year earlier. Single-family permits fell 8.7% to 772,000. Consensus figures are from the [Yahoo Finance live blog](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-wednesday-november-18-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-nvidia-anthropic-ipo-090613569.html).
- **Our interpretation:** Permits lead starts and are less noisy, so the permit drop is the more important signal. It matches Tuesday's weak NAHB index and Reuters' description of delayed large remodels in a sluggish housing market. It adds to the case that high mortgage rates are hurting housing, but it is unlikely to change a December Fed hike that is already almost fully priced.
