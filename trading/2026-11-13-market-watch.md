# U.S. Market Watch — November 13, 2026

**Session:** Friday, November 13, 2026 · Pre-open analysis, compiled at about 8:40 to 9:10 AM ET from reports published through about 8:35 AM ET and quotes captured at about 8:28 to 8:41 AM ET. Futures quotes come from CNBC's feed, which runs about 10 minutes behind, so they reflect only the first few minutes after the 8:30 AM ET PPI release. ET is UTC-5. Section 7 adds the after-close analysis, compiled at about 6:50 to 7:30 PM ET.

**Pre-open regime:** October PPI rose 0.3% on the month, below the 0.4% consensus, and 6.5% on the year against 6.6% expected. That is still the largest 12-month gain since November 2022. Prices excluding foods, energy and trade services rose 0.5%, matching September. S&P 500 futures are up about 0.6%, Nasdaq-100 futures about 0.6% and Russell 2000 futures about 0.65%, each higher than just before the release. The 2-year yield is down about 2 bp near 4.85%, while the 10-year is up about 3 bp near 5.13%. Nvidia is up about 2.3% near $170, extending Thursday's sharp recovery from the initial selloff after Jensen Huang's immediate resignation as CEO. Microsoft is down about 1%, having been down about 3% earlier, after a global Outlook and Teams outage that began Thursday afternoon. Cerebras is up about 12% and Modine about 35%. The VIX is down about 5% near 17.7.

**Pre-open summary:** Thursday's after-close note named PPI as the main event. The headline is a slight relief, but it is not a clean miss. Our interpretation: the drop in the 2-year and the gain in futures show traders treating the 0.3% as a modest relief after Tuesday's hot CPI. But the 0.5% rise excluding foods, energy and trade is firm, and the 10-year is still above 5.1%, so the report does not remove the case for a December hike. Equal weight (RSP, about +0.8%) and small caps are leading premarket, reversing Thursday's pattern. The S&P 500 is set to open near 7,640 (rough guide), between Wednesday's 7,619.38 close and Thursday's 7,652.76 high. The tests today are whether the small-cap and equal-weight bounce lasts past the open, whether the 10-year stays below its 5.158% overnight high, and whether Nvidia holds its bounce into its last session before Tuesday's earnings.

## 2. What is driving the open

### PPI: a softer headline, a firm underlying rate

- **Headline.** Final-demand PPI rose 0.3% in October, after an upwardly revised 1.4% in September (first reported as 1.3%) and 0.4% in August. The 12-month rise was 6.5%, "the largest 12-month rise since moving up 7.4 percent in November 2022" ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/ppi_11132026.htm)).
- **Underlying.** Final demand less foods, energy and trade services rose 0.5% for a second month and 4.9% over 12 months. Final-demand goods less foods and energy rose 0.5%, after 0.3%.
- **Mix.** Goods rose 0.5%: foods 1.3%, energy -0.1%. Services rose 0.2%, the fifth straight gain; three-quarters of that came from a 2.0% rise in transportation and warehousing, while trade margins fell 0.2%.
- **Consensus.** Dow Jones forecasts were 0.4% on the month and 6.6% on the year ([StockTitan preview](https://www.stocktitan.net/articles/october-2026-ppi-preview-yearly-rate)); core (excluding food and energy) was expected at 0.3% and 5.0% ([TradingKey](https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/stocks/us-stocks/262304609-nvda-nvidia-msft-us-ppi-tradingkey)). The core figure on that definition was not found in the BLS summary and was not checked.
- **Our interpretation:** Falling trade margins and a 0.1% decline in energy prices held down the headline. The 0.5% underlying rate is not soft, and the 2.0% rise in transportation and warehousing services was driven mainly by a 3.9% increase in truck-freight prices. That suggests the report trims the risk of a 50 bp hike rather than changing the case for 25 bp. The 2-year's small drop is consistent with that reading.

- **Fed commentary.** New York Fed President John Williams said Thursday that more hikes are likely (Reuters via [MLQ](https://mlq.ai/news/nasdaq-futures-dip-as-nvidia-heads-for-its-longest-weekly-losing-streak-since-2012-before-ppi-data/)), adding to Thursday's comments from Daly and Goolsbee. Neither topic is monetary policy.
- **Our interpretation:** Rates drove Thursday's selloff in small caps and equal weight. A lower 2-year helps, but a 10-year that is still rising shows long-dated bonds have not eased. The 10-year matters more for equal weight, housing and small caps.

### Nvidia: a bounce after the CEO-exit shock

- **Price.** Nvidia is up about 2.3% near $170.00 at 8:40 AM ET, against Thursday's $166.19 close. Its premarket range on Yahoo is about $167.16 to $170.96.
- **Leadership transition.** Nvidia announced Thursday that Jensen Huang had resigned as president, CEO and director, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress is serving as interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent successor; the company gave no reason for the resignation. Fiscal Q3 results remain scheduled for Tuesday at about 4:20 PM ET, followed by the call at 5:00 PM ET ([Nvidia press release filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000082/nvidiaannouncesceotransiti.htm)). Thursday's stock move—from a $148.80 opening low to a $166.19 close—shows the market absorbed much of the initial leadership shock, but the succession question remains unresolved.
- **Six weeks of losses.** Weekly closes on Yahoo show five straight weekly losses through November 6, from $233.95 on October 2 to $186.64. Thursday's close is about 11% below that level. Unless the stock rises above $186.64 today, it will post a sixth, which CNBC calls the longest weekly losing streak since 2012. The stock has lost about 29% over those six weeks and now has a smaller market value than Apple (CNBC via [MLQ](https://mlq.ai/news/nasdaq-futures-dip-as-nvidia-heads-for-its-longest-weekly-losing-streak-since-2012-before-ppi-data/)).
- **Analysts and flows.** D.A. Davidson's Gil Luria cut his target to $200 from $300 but kept a buy rating; Oppenheimer expects "fundamentals to take a backseat near term" (CNBC via MLQ). Vanda Research says retail investors bought a net $111 million in Thursday's first 30 minutes, "more than 40 times a normal start" ([Yahoo live blog](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-friday-november-13-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-nvidia-110151154.html)).
- **Our interpretation:** No additional company announcement came out overnight. The bounce fits continued retail buying and short-covering after Thursday's CEO-exit selloff, ahead of Tuesday's results. Holding Thursday's $166.19 close would extend the recovery; falling back below it would suggest the market is again pricing leadership risk.
