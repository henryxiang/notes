## 3. Early data and earnings

### Released before the open

**8:30 AM ET — October employment report.** Nonfarm payrolls rose 165,000, against about 59,000 (CNBC) to 65,000 (Reuters) expected ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm)). August was revised up to 186,000 from 133,000 and September to 60,000 from 29,000, a combined 84,000 increase. The unemployment rate fell to 4.1% from 4.2%, against 4.2% expected, with 7.1 million unemployed. Participation rose to 61.9% from 61.8%, and the employment-population ratio was 59.3%. Average hourly earnings rose 14 cents (0.4%) to $38.00, against 0.3% expected, and were up 3.1% from a year earlier, against about 2.9% expected (Yahoo live blog). The average workweek was unchanged at 34.4 hours.

By sector, professional and business services added 70,000, health care 42,000, transportation and warehousing 25,000 and manufacturing 19,000. Government, construction and retail changed little (BLS).

One soft spot was the number of people working part time for economic reasons, which rose by 339,000 to 4.8 million. These people wanted full-time work but had reduced hours or could not find it ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm)).

Our interpretation: this is a strong headline report, though the increase in involuntary part-time work adds a caution. The BLS says the 12-month average payroll gain is 48,000, and the Yahoo live blog says about 30,000 jobs a month are needed to keep the unemployment rate steady, so 165,000 is far above those benchmarks. Wage growth also picked up. Along with Thursday's low jobless claims (189,000) and falling unit labor costs, the data point to a solid labor market. That supports a December hike, but the increase in involuntary part-time work and the 0.7% drop in unit labor costs in the third quarter are counterarguments.
