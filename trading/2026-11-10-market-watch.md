## 2. What is driving the open

### A hot October CPI

- **The numbers.** The [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_11102026.htm) says CPI rose 0.4% in October, seasonally adjusted, after 0.6% in September. Core CPI rose 0.3% for the third month in a row. The Dow Jones consensus was 0.3% headline and 0.2% core (CNBC).
- **Details.** Shelter rose 0.3% and accounted for more than 30% of the headline gain. Energy rose 0.7% and gasoline 1.0%; before seasonal adjustment, gasoline fell 0.7%. Food rose 0.5%. Used cars and trucks fell 1.2%. Airfares and apparel rose (BLS).
- **No 12-month rates.** BLS did not collect October 2025 prices during the shutdown, so it cannot publish 12-month changes for most indexes ([BLS FAQ](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/additional-resources/2025-federal-government-shutdown-impact-cpi-faq.htm)). Yahoo says economists estimate that yearly inflation rose slightly.
- **Our interpretation:** The miss is only 0.1 point on each measure, but core has now run at 0.3% for three months, roughly a 3.5% to 4% annual pace (rough estimate). BLS reported increases across recreation, medical care, insurance, household furnishings and education even as used cars fell, so the core reading was not driven by one category. It is the result the Fed's hawks were watching for.

### Waller opens the door to 50 bp

- **The speech.** At the Bank of England Watchers Conference in London, [Waller](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/waller20261110a.htm) said he would support a 25 bp hike in December if the data came in as he expects. If October core CPI ran around 0.3% or higher, he said, "a 50 basis point hike in the policy rate should be considered." He added that "hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk, a risk I am not eager to take."
- **His inflation view.** PCE prices rose 3.7% in the 12 months through September and core PCE 3.1%. He is most concerned about how broad inflation has become, and says energy price pressures could be larger than previously thought and last well into 2027. He is also watching rising prices for AI-related high-tech goods.
- **Market pricing.** Before CPI, CME FedWatch put the odds of a 25 bp December hike above 80%. After the report, the odds were about 84% for 25 bp and about 15% for 50 bp, up from zero for 50 bp on Monday ([Yahoo live blog](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-tuesday-november-10-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-inflation-data-cpi-090627830.html)). Investing.com says the CPI and the strong jobs report essentially lock in a December hike.
- **Our interpretation:** Waller set a specific test, and the data passed it. The market has not fully priced 50 bp, and the 2-year's 14 bp jump suggests investors are moving that way. The FOMC decision is on December 9; the November jobs report and CPI come before it.

### Anthropic sets its IPO range

Anthropic set a range of $70 to $78 a share for a base offering of 825 million shares. At the top of the range, the base public offering could total about $64.4 billion and value the company at about $1 trillion; Bloomberg estimates the fully diluted value at about $1.4 trillion. The underwriters have an option to buy up to 123.75 million additional company shares, which would raise the public offering to about $74.0 billion at $78 if exercised in full. Anthropic is selling about 801.4 million of the base shares, while existing holders are selling about 23.6 million, so the company itself would receive less than the base offering value before fees. Seven companies—Alphabet's Google, Nvidia, Microsoft, AMD, Broadcom, Micron and TSMC—plan a separate $26 billion private placement. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are the lead underwriters ([SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026072484/anthropicpbc-sx1ax2.htm), [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/us-stock-futures-steady-after-techled-rebound-cpi-in-focus-5013763), [Yahoo live blog](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-tuesday-november-10-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-inflation-data-cpi-090627830.html)). Investing.com says earlier reports pointed to a valuation as high as $2 trillion. The filing says the sale will begin as soon as practicable after the registration statement becomes effective; a firm pricing date was not found. Our interpretation: an offering this large tests demand for AI stocks. It could also draw money away from listed AI names, and the lower valuation reflects last week's selloff.

- **Why.** CNBC cites President Trump calling new strikes on Iran "highly unlikely." No confirmation of Monday's reported Iranian concession on the Strait of Hormuz was found.
- **Our interpretation:** Cheaper oil would help November and December inflation, but the energy share of October CPI was already modest. For now the bond market is reacting to core inflation, not oil.
