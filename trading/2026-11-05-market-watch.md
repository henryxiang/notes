## 2. What is driving the open

### Chips sell off for a second day

- **Qualcomm.** Down about 9% (CNBC, 7:51 AM ET) to 11.7% (Yahoo quote at about $153.00, against a $173.19 close). Fiscal Q4 revenue of $10.44 billion and non-GAAP EPS of $2.17 were inside the guide and just above consensus, but the Q1 EPS guide of $2.15 to $2.35 was centered at $2.25, against $2.26 expected (FactSet, via CNBC). - **Arm.** Down about 2% (CNBC live blog) to more than 5% ([CNBC premarket movers](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/05/stocks-making-the-biggest-moves-premarket-shak-wen-pton-u.html), 7:51 AM ET) after adjusted EPS of $0.52 against $0.47 expected (LSEG).
- **The rest of the group.** Broadcom is down almost 3% and Nvidia nearly 2% (CNBC live blog). Bank of Vol has AMD, Micron and Marvell down about 2.3% and Sandisk 2.8%. Reuters has Marvell down 3.4%, Intel 2% and Dell 2.9%, and CoreWeave and Nebius down about 3%. Coherent is the exception: CNBC has it down 2%, Bank of Vol up 4.8%, after a beat.
- **Why.** CNBC ties the Asian chip selloff to concern about tighter data center rules after the midterms. TipRanks says Micron hit a three-month low on Wednesday.
- **Our interpretation:** The SOX closed at a new selloff low Wednesday, and this morning's moves suggest it opens lower again. The question for the session is whether the selling stays with memory, chip equipment and Qualcomm, or spreads to Nvidia. Nvidia traded as low as $199.68 Wednesday but recovered to close 13 cents above $200, so $200 remains the key area rather than an unbroken intraday floor.

### Platforms and software winners

- **AppLovin** is up about 19% to 21% after Q3 revenue of $2.18 billion against $2.07 billion expected and adjusted EBITDA of $1.84 billion against $1.73 billion (FactSet, via CNBC). Its Q4 guide was also above consensus (see the November 4 note).

- **Mega-caps.** Reuters reports Amazon, Meta and Alphabet each up more than 1%. Tesla and SpaceX are each down about 1% to 2%.
- **Our interpretation:** The market is still separating AI users and platforms from the companies that build and equip data centers. AppLovin and Unity's gains fit that pattern, as did Microsoft, Amazon and Apple's 5% to 7% gains on Wednesday.

### Strong U.S. labor and productivity data

- **Jobless claims** fell to 189,000 (see section 3). That is low, and it argues against the idea that the labor market is weakening quickly ahead of Friday's report.
- **Productivity** rose 3.6% and unit labor costs fell 0.7%. Our interpretation: strong output growth with falling labor costs per unit is the combination the Fed would like, because it eases wage-driven inflation pressure. The two reports pull in opposite directions for December hike odds: claims support a hike, while unit labor costs argue against one.

### Bank of England hikes to 4%

- **The decision.** The [Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy-summary-and-minutes/2026/november-2026) raised Bank Rate 25 bp to 4% in an 8-1 vote; Catherine Mann voted for 50 bp. It is the first hike since 2023 ([BusinessDay](https://www.businessday.co.za/world/europe/2026-11-05-bank-of-england-raises-rates-for-first-time-since-2023/)) and was about 98% priced (Bank of Vol).
- **Outlook.** The Bank expects CPI inflation, 3.5% in September, to reach about 4.5% in the first quarter of 2027 and about 5% in the second. It says policy "is not on a pre-set path." Greene, Lombardelli and Pill signaled more tightening is likely, while Taylor said this is not the start of a hiking cycle.
- **Energy.** The minutes say the Middle East conflict is in its ninth month, and Brent and U.K. gas futures were 38% and 81% higher than at the July report.
- **Our interpretation:** The hike was expected, so the main news is the hawkish lean of several members. It adds to the global case for higher policy rates while energy prices stay high, which matches the Fed's December hike pricing.

### Oil rebounds after Wednesday's plunge

- **Price action.** WTI is about $88.6 to $90 and Brent about $95.6 to $97 (see section 1). Oil fell to its lowest since August 31 on Wednesday.
- **Drivers.** OilPrice (via Yahoo) says investors are weighing whether Democrats' win could push President Trump toward a negotiated settlement with Iran. The IRGC's report of two tankers and a tugboat on fire after trying unauthorized routes through the Strait of Hormuz (Business Recorder) was not independently confirmed.
- **Our interpretation:** After an 8% one-day drop, a partial rebound is normal. A move back above $90 to $92 on WTI would cut into Wednesday's disinflation story for bonds and the rotation into consumer stocks.

### Policy

- **The Fed and the Senate.** Democrats' Senate control limits President Trump's ability to reshape the Federal Reserve, since Democrats would have to approve new Fed governors, according to UBS's Jonathan Pingle (Yahoo live blog).
- **Strategist caution.** Jordan Watson of Tandem Investment Advisors: "I wouldn't view [Wednesday's equity trading] as an all-clear signal" (CNBC live blog).

## 3. Early data and earnings

### Released before the open

**8:30 AM ET — Weekly jobless claims.** Initial claims for the week ended October 31 fell 6,000 to 189,000, against about 197,000 expected ([Department of Labor](https://www.dol.gov/ui/data.pdf); consensus from [Timothy Tanko's morning note](https://tradetank66.substack.com/p/daily-global-macro-morning-note-2f5)). The prior week was revised up to 195,000 from 194,000, and the 4-week average fell 2,250 to 194,750. Continuing claims for the week ended October 24 rose 23,000 to 1,718,000. Unadjusted claims of 176,698 compare with 216,026 a year earlier. The reading was the lowest since mid-July, according to the [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/039c8e6b655b7bc3dd5e2836bbb2ecc9). Our interpretation: layoffs remain very low; Challenger's [October job cuts](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-thursday-november-5-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-oil-falls-090519607.html) of 43,994, down 71% from a year earlier, tell the same story. Rising continuing claims suggest hiring remains slow, the "low hire, low fire" pattern.

**8:30 AM ET — Productivity and costs, Q3 preliminary.** Nonfarm productivity rose at a 3.6% annual rate, with output up 3.7% and hours up 0.1%. Unit labor costs fell 0.7%, as hourly compensation rose 2.9%. From a year earlier, productivity is up 2.1% and unit labor costs 2.4% ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/prod2.nr0.htm)). Q2 productivity was revised up to 2.1% from 1.4%, and Q2 unit labor costs to 1.9% from 1.2%. Consensus estimates ranged from about 2.6% to 3% (Yahoo live blog; secondary previews); no unit labor cost consensus was found. Manufacturing unit labor costs rose 2.7%. Our interpretation: productivity beat, and falling unit labor costs reduce the risk that wage growth feeds inflation. That is supportive for bonds, but the upward revision to Q2 costs offsets some of it.

### Why the market broke

- **Policy, round two.** Sen. Bernie Sanders renewed his call for a national data center moratorium after voters overwhelmingly backed local restrictions on Tuesday ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/11/62902720/bernie-sanders-data-center-moratorium-daniel-newman-demand-abroad)). [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/05/ai-data-center-election-backlash-concessions) counted 43 wins for the restrictive side across 44 ballot measures in 10 states. Many were zoning, approval or advisory questions rather than moratoriums, so they should not all be described as moratorium votes. Sanders and Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez introduced the AI Data Center Moratorium Act earlier this year. [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/markets/tech-stocks-plunge-ai-midterms-rcna607650) (updated 1:09 PM ET) says several Democratic winners for governor, attorney general, Senate and House pledged to slow or stop data center construction, and that Texas, Ohio, Michigan and Georgia are "of particular concern." New York Democrats have also proposed a broad pause ([Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/york-democrats-propose-sweeping-pause-150000390.html)); its details were not checked.
- **Industry response.** OpenAI said it would pay the full electricity and infrastructure costs of data centers it owns and operates and stop using nondisclosure agreements with public officials ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/05/openai-to-pay-for-data-center-power-after-election-shows-ai-concern.html)). Our interpretation: the move shows how seriously the industry takes the backlash. It raises costs for builders, and it did not stop the selloff.
- **Doubts about returns.** NBC says questions about whether AI will deliver enough productivity to justify its spending have "grown louder." Ed Yardeni: "I think it's an AI bubble losing some air very, very rapidly." The Macro Research Board said the AI shakeout "has much further to run" and advised diversifying away from tech (CNBC live blog).
- **Weak chip earnings.** Qualcomm's guide and Arm's results were the morning catalysts (section 2). Bernstein and Morgan Stanley kept neutral-type ratings on Qualcomm (CNBC live blog).
- **Other quotes.** Orion's Tim Holland said investors are "stepping away" until it is clearer how the midterms affect AI capex. Truist's Keith Lerner: "The rubber band is becoming increasingly stretched" (CNBC live blog). AMD's Lisa Su said the market can be "a little bit short-sighted" (Yahoo live blog); the full context was not checked.
- **Our interpretation:** Wednesday's market priced a slower U.S. buildout as bad for builders but fine for platforms and the broad economy. Today it is pricing the second-round effects: chip and memory earnings that depend on that spending, platforms whose growth stories depend on it, and an S&P 500 whose profit gains have been heavily concentrated in a small group of AI-linked companies. Apollo's Torsten Sløk has similarly argued that the AI capex boom is showing up in the sellers' margins but not yet in the broader S&P 493 ([Apollo](https://www.apollo.com/wealth/insights-news/insights/daily-spark/the-buyers-of-ai-are-still-waiting-for-the-payoff)). That makes this a valuation reset, not only a policy repricing.
