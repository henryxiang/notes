## 2. What is driving the open

### Nvidia: an investigation into its CEO

- **What happened.** On Sunday, Nvidia's board said Huang had been the target of a "foreign intelligence effort" and opened an independent investigation. The New York Times and the Financial Times reported that U.S. intelligence agencies believe Alice Huang, an executive at MegaSpeed, an Nvidia customer, has been passing information to Beijing ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/nvidia-jensen-huang-chinese-spy-investigation.html), 4:05 AM ET). In an email to staff, Huang admitted an affair and wrote that he had "fallen short" of his responsibilities. Nvidia says he "fully supports the review." China's Foreign Ministry called the spy story unsubstantiated.
- **Reactions.** President Trump called Huang an honorable man who likely has an explanation (CNBC). Yorkville Ives & Co.'s Dan Ives called it a "black eye moment" that "will spark multiple congressional inquiries at a minimum" but expects Huang to stay ([CNBC live blog](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/08/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)). Panmure Liberum's Joachim Klement says the impact is limited to Nvidia for now, but a weaker Huang could mean less Nvidia support for partner companies (CNBC). The Yahoo live blog notes there is no named successor; Huang has run Nvidia since co-founding it in 1993.
- **The stock.** Nvidia is down about 5.5% near $176.25 on Yahoo at 8:17 AM ET, after trading between about $174.49 and $181.00. That is below Thursday's $181.46 low and below $180. Nvidia reports fiscal third-quarter results on November 17.
- **Our interpretation:** The risk is about leadership and governance, plus possible political fallout in Washington, not about chip demand. That is why suppliers and rivals are up. The risk for the market is that Nvidia is about 7% of the S&P 500 (Yahoo live blog estimate), so a further drop would weigh on the index even if the rest of the market holds. New headlines, such as congressional inquiries or a leave of absence, which one investor close to Huang raised as a possibility (CNBC), could push the stock lower.

### A broad rebound in everything else

- **Mega-caps.** On Yahoo quotes at about 8:15 AM ET, Amazon is up about 3%, Alphabet about 2.9%, Meta about 2.4% and Microsoft about 2.1%. The Yahoo live blog says Google unveiled two new TPU chip versions to compete with Nvidia. Apple and Tesla are up about 0.3% to 0.5%.
- **Chips without Nvidia.** AMD is up about 3.5% after Oppenheimer upgraded it to outperform with a $600 target ([CNBC premarket movers](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/stock-market-movers-live-updates.html), page updated 8:01 AM ET). Broadcom is up about 3%, Lam Research and TSMC about 1.8%, and Micron about 0.8%. Intel is flat. SMH, which holds a large Nvidia position, is about flat.
- **Breadth.** RSP is up about 1% and IWM about 1.2%, both ahead of SPY's +0.7% and QQQ's +0.7%. Health care (XLV) is up about 1.1%, financials (XLF) about 0.9% and utilities (XLU) about 0.8%. Energy (XLE) is down about 0.6% with oil, and staples (XLP) are flat.
- **Strategists.** Reuters quotes Zaye Capital's Naeem Aslam saying the smaller Nasdaq gain shows investors "remain selective," and Capital Economics' Thomas Mathews warning the AI correction "could have much further to run." On Friday, Schwab's Liz Ann Sonders said retail investors "have not proven to be the dip buyers that they have been in the past" (CNBC live blog).
- **Our interpretation:** This is the reverse of Thursday, when chips dragged everything down. RSP leading SPY is the broadening signal that section 8 of the November 6 note said would point to a broader bottom. One premarket session does not confirm it; the test is whether RSP still leads at the close.

### Oil falls on Iran reports

- **The move.** WTI is down about 3% to 4% near $87 to $88 and Brent about 3.5% to 4.7% near $94 to $95. WTI traded as high as $91.32 overnight (Yahoo).
- **Why.** Bloomberg reports oil fell on an [unconfirmed report that Iran eased its Hormuz demands](https://bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-09/oil-falls-on-unconfirmed-report-that-iran-eased-hormuz-demands), reportedly dropping its demand to control the strait and charge transit fees. President Trump has said extensive strikes on Iran are "highly unlikely," depending on a "very strong agreement." But at about 7:30 AM ET, Rubio called Iran's latest demands "outrageous and ridiculous" and said the two sides remain far apart (CNBC live blog). A tanker incident in the strait was also reported by the UK Maritime Trade Operations agency.
- **Our interpretation:** Lower oil helps the inflation outlook a day before CPI and supports Monday's broad rally. But the drop rests on an unconfirmed report, and Rubio's comments show how quickly it could reverse. Brent fell about 17% last week, so oil is now well off its highs.

### Rates and the dollar

- **Yields.** The 10-year is about 4.85%, down about 1 bp, and the 2-year about 4.52%, unchanged (CNBC). The 3-year auction is at 1:00 PM ET.
- **Dollar and gold.** The dollar index is down about 0.5% and gold up about 1.4%.
- **Our interpretation:** Bonds are quiet ahead of CPI. The 2-year staying above 4.5% shows the market still expects a December hike after Friday's jobs report. A soft CPI on Tuesday would matter more to stocks than anything in today's calendar.
