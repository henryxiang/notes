**Session:** Monday, November 16, 2026 · Pre-open analysis, compiled at about 8:25 to 9:00 AM ET from reports published through about 8:30 AM ET and quotes captured at about 8:16 to 8:31 AM ET. **Pre-open regime:** The November Empire State manufacturing index fell 18 points to 7.8, well below the 17.6 consensus, and its prices-paid index fell 14 points to 45.9; yields barely moved in the first minutes after the 8:30 AM ET release. Chips are selling off again before Nvidia's results on Tuesday. **Pre-open summary:** Friday's after-close note said the week's chip rotation could keep unwinding, and the overnight session extended it. This is a chip and AI selloff, not a rates selloff: yields are lower, equal weight (RSP) is about flat, and defensive sectors (health care, staples) are higher premarket. Our interpretation: the market is cutting exposure to the Nvidia supply chain before a report that will be the company's first without Huang, while the CEO question is moving in a new direction (talk of a return) rather than toward a resolution. The S&P 500 is set to open near 7,520 (rough guide), between Friday's 7,499.40 low and Thursday's 7,550.84 low. The Nasdaq-100 is indicated near Friday's low of about 27,610. The tests today are whether the S&P 500 holds 7,499.40, whether the SOX holds 9,829 (Friday's low), whether Nvidia holds Thursday's $148.80 low, and whether Chairman Warsh's noon remarks say anything about policy.

## 2. What is driving the open

### Chips and AI: the selloff spreads to the supply chain

- **Premarket moves.** Micron is down about 6.5%, Marvell about 6.2%, Arm about 5.4%, Intel about 5.4%, Lam Research about 5.2%, TSMC about 5.2%, Applied Materials about 5.0%, AMD about 5.6% and Broadcom about 4.3%. SMH is down about 4.1%, which points to a SOX open near 9,735 (rough guide), below Friday's 9,829 low and below 10,000.
- **AI infrastructure.** CoreWeave is down about 7.5%, Nebius about 8.3%, Oracle about 3.1% and Cerebras about 5.0%. Modine is down about 2.0% and Cisco about 1.9%.
- **Context.** AMD and Broadcom gained about 19% and 18% last week even after Friday's losses, and the SOX about 8%, while Nvidia fell about 12.7% ([November 13 note](2026-11-13-market-watch.md)). Today's selling hits the names that had been bought as alternatives to Nvidia, not just Nvidia itself.
- **Our interpretation:** Taiwan's 6% drop shows the selling has moved from "who replaces Nvidia's lead" to "how much of the AI supply chain is exposed to Nvidia." Memory (Micron, SK hynix) and foundry (TSMC) are among the hardest hit, consistent with that. Whether U.S. buyers step in at the open, as they did on Thursday, is the main question for the day.

### Nvidia: comeback talk, not resolution

- **Price.** Nvidia is down about 4.2% near $156.09 at 8:26 AM ET, against Friday's $162.85 close. Its premarket range on Yahoo is about $155.60 to $162.97. Thursday's low was $148.80.
- **The FT report.** The Financial Times reported on Sunday that Huang has discussed returning as CEO with confidants. A supporter described a "war room" of Silicon Valley executives being assembled to lobby Nvidia's board, including by seeking White House support (via [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/nvidia-stock-steady-as-jensen-huang-reportedly-weighs-ceo-comeback-musk-backs-return-015644730.html)). No board statement on the report was found.
- **Musk and the petition.** Elon Musk said on X on Friday that he is signing a petition to reinstate Huang, and on Saturday that many of Nvidia's supply-chain deals were handshake agreements with Huang rather than written contracts (Yahoo Finance). Stocktwits reports that the petition has more than 150,000 signers and that Gene Munster raised his odds of Huang's return to 51% from 10% ([Stocktwits via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/dow-p-500-nasdaq-futures-020421221.html)). Wedbush's Dan Ives called a return "ridiculous" given legal, board and Washington scrutiny. None of these comments were checked against the originals.
- **Analysts.** At least 10 analysts have cut the stock from buy to hold, citing Washington scrutiny and the succession question (Yahoo Finance).
- **Tuesday's report.** Fiscal Q3 2027 results are due Tuesday at about 4:20 PM ET, with the call at 5:00 PM ET ([Nvidia filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000082/nvidiaannouncesceotransiti.htm)). Consensus is about $108.93 billion in revenue, up about 91% from a year earlier, and adjusted EPS of $2.47 ([TipRanks](https://www.tipranks.com/news/stock-market-today-nov-16-u-s-stocks-mixed-ahead-of-nvidia-results-and-anthropic-ipo)). A TipRanks headline says options price about a 9% move; not checked.
- **Our interpretation:** The comeback talk adds a second, opposite outcome to the succession question, which widens rather than narrows the range of results. It also keeps the China and Washington angle in the news, which is the part Asia is pricing. Musk's comment about handshake deals, if taken seriously, turns a governance issue into a question about supply contracts, and that may be what memory and foundry stocks are reacting to.

### Rates and the Fed

- **Curve.** The 2-year is down about 2 bp near 4.85% and the 10-year about 1 bp near 5.14%, off Friday's 5.174% intraday high. TLT is up about 0.1%.
- **Fed odds.** About 94% for a 25 bp hike on December 9 (CME FedWatch via TipRanks).
- **Today.** Governor Michael Barr spoke on AI and financial stability at 5:00 AM ET in Milan, and Chairman Kevin Warsh gives opening remarks at a San Francisco Fed productivity conference at 12:00 PM ET ([Federal Reserve calendar](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/2026-november.htm)).
- **Barr on AI financing.** Barr said on-balance-sheet debt of the seven largest AI firms is expected to exceed $800 billion by year-end and cited a Fed estimate that their maximum nominal off-balance-sheet obligations exceed $3 trillion. He also highlighted circular financing, collateral guarantees and the risk that leverage could amplify an AI repricing ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/barr20261116a.htm)).
- **Our interpretation:** Bonds are acting as a modest haven this morning, the opposite of last week, when yields rose every day of the selloff in rate-sensitive stocks. Barr's financial-stability warnings reinforce the market's focus on leveraged AI infrastructure, but they are not a monetary-policy signal. Warsh's remarks are billed as opening remarks on productivity, so a policy signal is possible but not likely.

### Empire State survey: a miss on activity, softer prices

- **Headline.** The general business conditions index fell 18 points to 7.8 in November, from 25.5 in October, its highest since December 2021 ([New York Fed](https://www.newyorkfed.org/survey/empire/empiresurvey_overview)). Consensus was 17.6 ([TipRanks](https://tipranks.com/news/november-u-s-factory-report-tests-inflation-pressure-monday-as-markets-price-in-december-rate-hike)).
- **Details.** New orders fell to 3.0 from 23.2 and shipments to 8.8. Employment rose to 18.1, its highest since June 2022, and the workweek index to 13.5. Capital spending plans rose nine points to 23.0, the highest since December 2022, and future business conditions eight points to 28.9.
- **Prices.** Prices paid fell 14 points to 45.9 and prices received three points to 27.2. The New York Fed calls the pace of price increases "slowed but remained elevated."
- **Reaction.** At about 8:31 AM ET the 2-year was about 4.85% and the 10-year about 5.14%, essentially unchanged from just before the release (CNBC). Futures quotes after the release were not yet available on CNBC's delayed feed.
- **Our interpretation:** Weaker orders and softer input prices lean against further hikes, but strong hiring and a regional survey's limited weight mean it is unlikely to move December odds from about 94%. It does not change the chip-driven picture this morning.
