## 2. What is driving the open

### Democrats take Congress; markets read it as gridlock

- **Results.** The CNBC Election Desk and the [AP](https://apnews.com/article/stock-markets-ai-earnings-oil-4d12764c0b3ce38ffde3077712811182) project Democratic control of the Senate and the House; some races were still being counted (see section 7 of the [November 3 note](2026-11-03-market-watch.md)). The new Congress takes office in January, and the President keeps the veto.
- **The overnight swing.** Nasdaq-100 futures fell as much as 2.4% and S&P 500 futures 1.4% as results came in, then recovered after President Trump said he would accept the results (TradingKey). The Nasdaq-100 overnight low of 27,615.75 was its lowest since July 30 (CNBC).
- **Market framing.** Jay Woods of Freedom Capital Markets: "there will be gridlock and historically this is good for the market." Andrew Slimmon of Morgan Stanley Investment Management said markets "like gridlock — they don't like a party with too much power." Edward Jones' Angelo Kourkafas said AI and geopolitics remain "the primary forces driving markets" (CNBC).
- **Policy themes.** Yahoo's live blog says investors are watching for action on housing and drug prices, and that the new majorities could open investigations into the President's economic allies.
- **Our interpretation:** Futures are pricing the result as a reduced chance of big new policy, not as a growth shock. That favors domestic cyclicals and small caps over the AI trade, which was the weakest part of the market's reaction.

### AI data-center stocks are the main loser

- **Movers.** CoreWeave is down about 7% (CNBC, 7:52 AM ET) to 8% (CNBC live blog), Iren about 6%, Nebius about 5% to 6%, and Applied Digital about 3% ([Investing.com](https://ca.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/premarket-movers-trump-media-ai-infrastructure-stocks-fall-amd-jumps-4929238), 7:06 AM ET). Oracle is down about 1.2% after Tuesday's 5.85% gain (Investing.com). Yahoo's live blog has Nvidia and Micron down more than 1%.
- **Why.** CNBC cites Democrats' critical positions on data center expansion and local rejections of data center projects. Investing.com adds possible tighter regulation, changes to technology tax incentives and more antitrust scrutiny.
- **Texas.** Democratic governor-elect Gina Hinojosa called for a broad moratorium on data center construction (Yahoo live blog). Wells Fargo estimates that Texas accounts for about 27% of planned U.S. data center gigawatts (CNBC live blog), not 27% of currently operating capacity. Incumbent Governor Greg Abbott had already [halted new state approvals](https://gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-directs-tceq-to-halt-data-center-permits) pending an audit, so the election risk is about the breadth and duration of restrictions rather than the first pause. How a Hinojosa moratorium would work, and whether the state legislature would pass one, was not checked.
- **Tesla and SpaceX.** Tesla is down almost 6% and SpaceX (SPCX) more than 7% ([CNBC premarket movers](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/04/stocks-making-the-biggest-moves-premarket-djt-tsla-crwv-nvo.html), 7:52 AM ET). CNBC ties the drop to the data-center concerns. TradingKey adds possible congressional scrutiny of the companies' government contracts and of a possible Tesla–SpaceX merger.
- **Our interpretation:** This is the "Democratic sweep" line in Tuesday's reaction matrix playing out for AI stocks, but not for the broader market. The cost falls on companies building or financing data centers more than on chip designers; Nvidia and Micron are down only about 1%.

### AMD's beat fades premarket

- **Results.** Q3 revenue was about $13.4 billion (+45%), above the $13.3 billion top of the guide range. Non-GAAP EPS was $2.09 against $1.95 expected (LSEG), and the Q4 revenue guide is about $17 billion, plus or minus 3%, against about $16.23 billion expected. Full details are in section 7 of the [November 3 note](2026-11-03-market-watch.md).
- **Reaction.** AMD was up about 7% after hours and more than 7% in early premarket trading (Investing.com, 7:06 AM ET). CNBC had it "little changed" at 7:52 AM ET, and Yahoo's live blog says it fell before the bell after initially rising.
- **Our interpretation:** A beat and a strong guide that cannot hold a gain is a bearish sign for chips. The likeliest explanation is the policy overhang on AI infrastructure, but that is not established. AMD's $483.90 close is the line to watch.

### Rotation winners: renewables, health care, cyclicals

- **Renewables.** First Solar is up about 8% to 8.5%, Sunrun about 6% to 8.6%, Canadian Solar about 7%, Plug Power about 6% to 10.6%, and SolarEdge about 3% (CNBC; Investing.com). The sources cite expectations of more supportive policy.
- **Health care.** Molina is up about 6.3%, HCA about 7.2%, Centene about 5% and UnitedHealth about 1.8% (CNBC live blog). No specific cause was given. Our interpretation: managed care and hospitals tend to benefit from expectations of steadier government health spending; that is not the sources' stated reason.
- **Trump Media** is down about 7% to 11% as investors unwind trades tied to the President's political fortunes (Investing.com; CNBC). TipRanks reports defense stocks lower without figures.
- **Our interpretation:** The leadership change matches the futures: the Dow and Russell 2000 lead, while the Nasdaq-100 lags. Tuesday had tech leadership with weak breadth. Today's open, if it holds, would reverse that, the third change in leadership in three sessions.

### Rates ease; Treasury holds near-term auction sizes

- **Treasuries.** The 10-year is about 4.915% to 4.93%, 2 to 4 bp below Tuesday's 4.95% close, and the 2s/10s curve is about 2 bp flatter (ZeroHedge; Yahoo). Our interpretation: lower yields alongside a weaker dollar and gold above $4,000 are consistent with markets pricing less fiscal expansion under divided government. The sources do not attribute the move.
- **Refunding.** Treasury will sell $125 billion of securities next week to refund about $97 billion maturing November 15: a $58 billion 3-year note on November 9, a $42 billion 10-year note on November 10 and a $25 billion 30-year bond on November 12, all at 1:00 PM ET. Treasury "anticipates maintaining nominal coupon and FRN auction sizes for the next several quarters" and plans buybacks of up to $70 billion for liquidity support over the coming quarter ([Treasury refunding statement](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0678)). The sizes match August's refunding ([DRL Group](https://www.drlgroup.net/treasury-releases-quarterly-auction-schedule/)). However, Treasury removed the word "at least" from its prior guidance, giving it more flexibility to change sizes in 2027, and will convert the 7-year note from monthly new issues to quarterly new issues with two reopenings beginning in March. Our interpretation: unchanged near-term coupon sizes avoid an immediate supply shock, but the softer guidance makes the announcement less clearly supportive for longer-term yields than the draft first suggested.
- **Fed odds.** Secondary sources put CME FedWatch odds of a December 9 hike at about 79%, up from about 72% to 76% before Tuesday's JOLTS report ([Vantage Markets](https://www.vantagemarkets.com/market-analysis/why-gold-stalled-fed-hike-bets-xauusd-analysis-november-4-2026/)). CappNotes cited 78% Tuesday night. [CME FedWatch](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/interest-rates/cme-fedwatch-tool.html) was not checked directly, and no post-election reading was found.

### Oil slips on Iran comments

- **Price action.** WTI is about $94.4 to $95 (-1% to -1.8%), below Monday's $95.16 low, and Brent about $101.2 to $102.6 (Yahoo; ZeroHedge; TradingKey).
- **Iran.** ZeroHedge reports President Trump said the U.S. has "no immediate plans to strike Iran," and that Tehran and Washington are reviewing new mediator proposals to avoid a return to full-scale war. It also reports an IRGC claim that four tankers were destroyed after hitting mines south of the Strait of Hormuz. That claim was not confirmed by a second source, and oil's decline suggests markets are discounting it. TipRanks describes the talks as Qatar-mediated.
- **Our interpretation:** Oil is back below where it was before the Qeshm reports, so diplomacy remains the market's base case. An independently confirmed tanker attack would be the main upside risk.

## 3. Early data and earnings

### Released before the open

**8:15 AM ET — ADP employment, October.** Private employers added 86,000 jobs, against a Dow Jones estimate of 75,000 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/04/private-companies-added-86000-jobs-in-october-slightly-higher-than-expected-adp-says.html); [ADP release](https://mediacenter.adp.com/2026-11-04-ADP-National-Employment-Report-Private-Sector-Employment-Increased-by-86,000-Jobs-in-October)). September was revised down to 86,000 from 90,000. Pay for job-stayers rose 3.0% from a year earlier and for job-changers 4.8%. Trade, transportation and utilities added 39,000 and education and health services 28,000. Professional and business services lost 27,000, a third straight monthly decline. Other consensus figures were lower: about 67,000 (ZeroHedge) and 70,000 (Yahoo). Our interpretation: a modest beat with an equal downward revision is roughly neutral. Steady 3.0% stayer pay does not add to Tuesday's wage-pressure signal from quits.

**8:30 AM ET — Trade, September.** The goods and services deficit narrowed to $105.0 billion from a revised $105.7 billion, against estimates of about $107.9 billion (ZeroHedge) to $108 billion ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/balance-of-trade)). Exports rose 1.9% to $321.5 billion and imports 1.2% to $426.6 billion. The goods deficit was $135.9 billion ([BEA release](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/us-international-trade-goods-and-services-september-2026)). The nominal headline was a little better than expected, but the release's inflation-adjusted goods deficit widened by $3.5 billion to $119.5 billion. Our interpretation: the report is not an unambiguous positive for third-quarter GDP revisions; it is unlikely to move markets on the day after the election.
