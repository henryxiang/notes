**Pre-open regime:** The AI selloff is getting broader and faster. S&P 500 futures are down about 1.9%, Nasdaq-100 futures about 2.8%, Dow futures about 1.3% and Russell 2000 futures about 1.2%. The VIX is up about 17% near 30.9. Nvidia is down about 5.4% near $147 and SMH about 4.8%, after Tuesday's 7.9% drop in the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index. Selling has now reached the mega-cap AI platforms: Alphabet is down about 4.2%, Oracle about 5.3%, Microsoft about 3.2% and Amazon about 3.0%. Overnight, the Kospi fell 6.2%, the Taiex 4.8% and the Nikkei 2.8%. Dell is down about 5.6% despite a large beat and raise, and Ambarella is down about 34% on its guidance. Anthropic (ANT) is down about 4.6% near $78.15, further below its $83 offer price. Trade adds a second worry: reports say the administration will announce new tariffs on China, the EU and about 14 other economies, with rates and timing not yet confirmed.

**Pre-open summary:** Tuesday's rotation out of chips has turned into broader selling. The S&P 500 is indicated near 7,064 (rough guide), about 27 points above its 7,037.04 correction line. Monday's rally had cleared that line; at 7,064 the index would keep less than half of Monday's 327-point gain. The Nasdaq-100 is indicated near 25,467, about 2% above its 20% bear-market line (about 24,980). Three things are different from Tuesday. First, the defensive offset is smaller: health care and the equal-weight S&P 500 (RSP) are slightly lower rather than higher, and only staples are clearly up. Second, the selling now includes the platforms that buy AI hardware (Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle), not just the suppliers. Third, a tariff headline gives the selling a reason beyond positioning. Treasurys are firmer (TLT up about 0.8%), and gold is lower again, so this does not look like a classic flight to havens. Instead it looks like a market reducing risk into a holiday. The 8:30 AM ET data did not change the picture much: core PCE inflation was 0.1 point above consensus at 3.3%, but GDP growth was revised up to 4.3% and jobless claims fell to 189,000. SPY slipped about another 0.2% after the release and TLT was little changed. The tests today are whether the S&P 500 holds 7,037.04, whether the Nasdaq-100 holds 25,000, whether Nvidia holds $145.98 (Thursday's close) and $125.53 (Friday's pre-reinstatement close) and whether ANT holds $75.61 (Tuesday's low).

### Tariffs: new duties expected on 16 trading partners

- **The investigation.** In March, USTR opened Section 301 investigations into "structural excess capacity" in 16 economies: China, the EU, Singapore, Switzerland, Norway, Indonesia, Malaysia, Cambodia, Thailand, South Korea, Vietnam, Taiwan, Bangladesh, Mexico, Japan and India ([USTR](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/march/ustr-initiates-section-301-investigations-relating-structural-excess-capacity-and-production)).
- **What is reported.** Reuters reported, in a story updated at about 6:11 AM ET today, that the administration is set to announce duties "specifically tailored to each country," with no date given ([Reuters via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/trump-administration-set-unveil-tariffs-161914689.html)). The duties are meant to replace the "Liberation Day" tariffs the Supreme Court struck down in February. Mexico and Norway are unlikely to face immediate levies. Caps in existing trade deals are expected to hold: 15% for the EU, Japan and South Korea, and 19% for Malaysia. China's second-term tariffs are capped at 20%, which Reuters says leaves about 7.5 points of room, assuming the truce that expires January 10 is extended. Politico, crediting the Wall Street Journal, had said the proposals could come Tuesday night, and that the rates would be proposals subject to hearings and comments ([Politico via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/trump-administration-tees-tariff-hikes-161851136.html)).
- **Not yet official.** USTR's press-release page showed no determination as of about 8:20 AM ET ([USTR](https://ustr.gov/about-us/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases)). Rates for India, Vietnam and the other economies have not been reported. Reuters' premarket story names the tariffs as one reason for the decline in futures ([Reuters via the Globe and Mail](https://theglobeandmail.com/investing/markets/inside-the-market/market-news/article-premarket-world-stocks-fall-as-new-us-tariffs-loom-ai-rebound-fizzles)), and Korean and Taiwanese reports cite them as well.
- **Other policy news.** The Commerce Department's addition of seven Southeast Asian entities to the Entity List, including three Megaspeed entities, is published in the Federal Register today (see the November 24 note). Investing.com reports that the list also includes Alibaba's Singapore unit, which Alibaba called "erroneous" (via [Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/asian-stocks-slide-ai-selloff-040525614.html)).
- **Our interpretation:** If the caps hold, the new tariffs mostly restore duties the Supreme Court struck down rather than raising them well beyond earlier levels. That limits the economic shock. The market impact comes from the timing: the headline arrives while investors are already selling, and it hits Korea, Taiwan and Japan, which are central to AI supply chains. The most uncertainty is for economies where no cap has been reported, such as India, Vietnam and Taiwan.

### Tariffs: proposals, not yet in effect

- **What was announced.** USTR proposed excess-capacity tariffs on 14 of the 16 economies it investigated: China, the EU, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, India, Vietnam, Singapore, Switzerland, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand. Mexico and Norway were left out for now ([Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/world-news/us-proposes-new-tariffs-on-14-trading-partners-to-fight-excess-factory-capacity-5050714); [RTÉ](https://www.rte.ie/news/us/2026/1125/1600630-us-tariffs/)).
- **Rates.** USTR's [official announcement](https://www.ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/november/ustr-makes-determinations-and-proposes-actions-14-section-301-investigations-relating-structural) proposes rates of up to 35% for Vietnam, 25% for India, 20% for Singapore and certain Chinese goods, and 18% for Malaysia, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia and Thailand. It proposes 15% for the EU and up to 15% for Japan, South Korea, Switzerland and Taiwan. The treatment of existing duties and exemptions varies by economy and product; these figures should not be assumed to be flat additional tariffs. China also has a proposed 10% rate for certain listed goods. USTR proposes considering 20% for Vietnam and 17% for India if qualifying trade agreements are reached.
- **Timing.** Written comments are due January 5, 2027, and hearings start January 25, according to USTR. The duties are proposals and are not yet in effect; implementation timing has not been finalized.
- **Our interpretation:** The proposal was roughly in line with the morning reports, with the deal caps holding for the major allies. Industrials, materials and retailers all finished higher, which suggests the market saw the outcome as manageable. The main uncertainty is for economies without deals (Vietnam, India).

- **Our interpretation:** The data were firm and the Beige Book's wage and price language leans hawkish, yet the bond market rallied. That fits a market buying Treasurys as a hedge against the equity selloff rather than one repricing the Fed. A stronger housing report and lower yields gave rate-sensitive cyclicals a reason to lead the rebound.
