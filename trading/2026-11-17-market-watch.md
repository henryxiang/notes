## 2. What is driving the open

### Retail sales: in line on the headline, firmer underneath

- **Headline.** October retail and food services sales rose 0.2% to $748.2 billion (±0.4%), in line with the 0.2% consensus. They were up 6.8% from a year earlier. The Census Bureau says the 90% confidence interval for the monthly change includes zero. September's 0.8% gain was unrevised ([Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/retail/marts/www/marts_current.pdf)).
- **Excluding autos** sales rose 0.6% against a 0.4% forecast; consensus figures are from the [Yahoo Finance live blog](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-tuesday-november-17-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-nvidia-earnings-091546152.html). Excluding autos and gas, sales rose 0.5%. Motor vehicle and parts dealers fell 1.6%, and gasoline stations rose 1.5%.
- **Control group.** Our calculation from the release's seasonally adjusted Table 1 puts the control group (sales excluding autos, gas, building materials and food services) up about 0.6%. No consensus for the control group was found.
- **Categories.** Nonstore retailers rose 1.2%, miscellaneous stores 4.7% and health and personal care 1.5%. Furniture fell 2.3%, and clothing and sporting goods each fell 0.7%. Food services and drinking places rose 0.2%.
- **Import prices.** October import prices rose 1.1%, above the 0.7% consensus and matching September's revised 1.1% increase. Nonfuel import prices rose 0.8%, while fuel import prices rose 3.6%. Export prices rose 1.1% ([Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ximpim.nr0.htm)).
- **Reaction.** The 10-year was about 5.12% before the 8:30 AM ET releases and about 5.14% at 8:45 AM ET. The 2-year rose to about 4.85%, near its overnight high. Because retail sales and import prices were released simultaneously, the yield move cannot be attributed cleanly to either report alone.
- **Our interpretation:** The retail-sales headline was held down by autos, while the categories that feed into GDP were solid. The hotter-than-expected import-price reading adds an inflationary signal. Together, they fit a Fed that is still hiking: Musalem's call for more hikes (below), a firm consumer and rising import costs all lean against any idea of a pause after December. The combination is a modest negative for small caps and rate-sensitive groups, not a shock.

### Chips and AI: a bounce before Nvidia

- **Premarket moves.** Marvell is up about 3.6%, Lam Research about 1.7%, AMD about 1.5%, Applied Materials about 1.4%, Arm about 1.3%, Micron about 1.2% and Broadcom about 0.9%. TSMC is down about 0.4%, after Taiwan's second day of losses, and Intel is down about 1.4%. SMH is up about 1.0%, which points to a SOX open near 9,710 (rough guide). That would be above Monday's 9,587.11 low but below its 9,818.76 high.
- **Optical networking.** Coherent is up about 8.0%, Applied Optoelectronics about 7.6%, Fabrinet about 5.6%, Lumentum about 4.8% and Corning about 2.7%. Reuters reported that the administration plans to go ahead with restricting imports of new Chinese optical-transceiver models, despite lobbying against the plan by Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft, CoreWeave and Cisco ([Investing.com, citing Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/coherent-lumentum-jump-as-us-presses-ahead-with-china-equipment-curbs-5032701)). Counterpoint Research estimates China holds about two-thirds of the global market. The FCC could still modify or drop the rule.
- **AI infrastructure.** Nebius is up about 2.1%, CoreWeave about 0.3% and Cerebras about 0.5%; Oracle is down about 0.5%.
- **Sentiment.** Bank of America's November fund manager survey (November 6 to 12) showed the net share of investors overweight tech falling to 12% from 37% in October, the largest cut in about two decades (Bloomberg via SWI swissinfo.ch).
- **Our interpretation:** After a 5.3% drop in the SOX and a record cut in tech positioning, a bounce before a major report is not surprising. The optical rally is a policy trade on U.S. suppliers gaining share, not a vote of confidence in AI demand. Whether chips hold the bounce into the afternoon, rather than fading as they did Monday after 9:40 AM ET, is the main intraday test.

### Nvidia: results after the close

- **Price.** Nvidia is up about 0.7% near $157.26 at 8:45 AM ET, against Monday's $156.14 close. Monday's low was $155.01, and Thursday's post-resignation low was $148.80.
- **Expectations.** Consensus is about $109.15 billion in revenue and adjusted EPS of $2.47, against $57 billion and $1.30 a year earlier (Yahoo Finance live blog). Visible Alpha expects about $109.3 billion, including about $101.8 billion from data centers. The company's prior guidance was $108 billion, plus or minus 2%, with a non-GAAP gross margin of about 74% (TradingKey). UBS cited an options-implied move of about 7.8% (Reuters via MarketScreener). Monday's note cited about 9% from a TipRanks headline.
- **Leadership.** Interim CEO Colette Kress, who is also CFO, will host the call. Ian Buck and Arm CEO Rene Haas have been reported as candidates for the permanent job. The FT reported that Huang has discussed a comeback, while CNBC and Reuters reported Monday that the board is not considering it (Yahoo Finance live blog). Bloomberg reports that Nvidia formed a board committee to oversee national security and export controls (via SWI swissinfo.ch). That was not checked against an Nvidia filing.
- **Holdings.** Nvidia's third-quarter 13F shows SpaceX became its largest disclosed holding, valued at $19.68 billion, just above its $19.37 billion Intel position ([SEC information table](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000084/xslForm13F_X02/information_table.xml)). The 25% reduction in its Intel share count is based on comparison with its second-quarter filing.
- **Our interpretation:** The numbers are expected to be strong. Saxo's Neil Wilson said that "is not necessarily going to underpin the market." The swing factors are likely the fiscal Q4 guide, any comment on the remote-access rule's effect on overseas customers, and whether the board says anything concrete about the CEO search.

### Rates and the Fed

- **Curve.** The 2-year is up about 3 bp near 4.85% and the 10-year about 2 bp near 5.14%. TLT is about flat (-0.04%).
- **Musalem.** St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem said further removal of accommodation will be needed soon and that the economy will likely require several rate increases before spring 2027. He put the needed real policy rate "solidly" between 2% and 2.5%, versus his estimate of 1% to 1.5% now. He said the adjustment should ideally come from both lower inflation and a higher nominal rate, although the nominal rate may need to do most of the work ([St. Louis Fed](https://www.stlouisfed.org/from-the-president/remarks/2026/remarks-on-us-economy-and-monetary-policy-argentina)). Musalem is not a 2026 FOMC voter.
- **Warsh.** Chairman Kevin Warsh opened the Fed's Productivity and Jobs Task Force conference on Monday. He said the Fed's role is neither to advocate for nor oppose AI, but to prepare for outcomes ranging from broad gains to job displacement or a productivity slump ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/warsh20261116a.htm)).
- **Today.** Governor Christopher Waller speaks on payments at 9:10 AM ET ([Federal Reserve calendar](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/2026-november.htm)). Industrial production for October is due at 9:15 AM ET (consensus +0.1%, prior +0.7%), and the NAHB housing index at 10:00 AM ET (consensus 29).
- **Our interpretation:** Bonds were a haven overnight but gave that up after the data. Musalem has called for hikes since September, so his remarks are no surprise. Still, he is pricing a path well beyond December, which matters more for the 2-year than for stocks today. A 10-year back above 5.174% would bring last week's rate pressure back on top of the chip question.
