## 2. What is driving the tape

### AI-credit concerns remain the primary equity risk

The pressure has shifted from questioning AI demand to questioning who can finance the buildout profitably. CoreWeave was down about 4% pre-market after stabilizing Thursday; Nebius and Iren were down roughly 2%, while Micron and Broadcom were also indicated lower. The market is distinguishing cash-funded hyperscalers and profitable chip suppliers from leveraged data-center operators whose returns must exceed borrowing costs above 5%. ([Reuters report](https://www.aol.com/articles/wall-st-futures-mixed-tech-110148000.html))

This is an important distinction: strong AI demand can coexist with weak AI-infrastructure equities if financing costs, dilution, and credit risk overwhelm revenue growth. Watch whether good news can lift the group. Continued selling despite favorable operating updates would be a bearish signal.

### Warsh did not validate the most hawkish rate fears

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said overnight that price stability remains essential and that the Fed removed some accommodation in September, but he declined to prejudge the October or December meetings. Markets interpreted the lack of a direct hawkish signal as mildly supportive, reducing expectations for an October hike even after this week's hot CPI and PPI releases. ([AP on Warsh's remarks](https://apnews.com/article/4c80750c113a82a464a46f58a4fdab43))

The result is an uneasy combination: inflation data argue for another hike, but the bond market's rally and Warsh's restraint are limiting the immediate pressure on equities. The 10-year yield remains the cleanest referee.

### Overseas technology recovered, but global risk is uneven

South Korea's Kospi rebounded 2.1% after Thursday's 7.6% collapse, and Japan's Nikkei rose 1.4%. However, Indonesia's Jakarta Composite plunged more than 11% after MSCI warned that the market could lose emerging-market status. The Asian rebound helps U.S. semiconductor sentiment at the margin, but the Indonesian shock is a reminder that liquidity remains fragile. ([AP global markets report](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-us-ai-oil-tech-warsh-d2655f64db7ec8ae450525d58f97b628))

## 5. Trading framework for the open

### Base case: rotation continues

The most likely setup is a stable or modestly positive S&P 500, a firmer Dow, and continued two-way volatility in the Nasdaq. Falling yields can support a technology bounce, but they do not resolve the financing problem in leveraged AI names.

### Confirmation signals

1. **10-year Treasury yield:** A sustained move below roughly 5.05% would improve the odds of a Nasdaq recovery. A reversal above 5.15% after the data would pressure long-duration growth.
2. **Nasdaq versus Dow:** Continued Dow outperformance confirms rotation. Simultaneous weakness in both indexes would signal that selling is broadening.
3. **AI-credit complex:** CoreWeave, Nebius, Iren, Oracle, and credit-sensitive data-center names are the stress gauges. Stabilization matters more than a brief opening bounce.
4. **Semiconductor breadth:** Watch whether Micron, Broadcom, Nvidia, TSM, and the SOX index can turn positive together.
5. **Regional banks:** Strong results and constructive credit commentary could extend value leadership; weak guidance would remove an important source of market support.
6. **Oil:** A renewed rise would reinforce the inflation problem and could push yields higher even if equities initially ignore it.

### Scenario map

| Scenario | Confirmation | Practical posture |
|---|---|---|
| Constructive rotation | Dow and banks lead; breadth positive; NQ lags without breaking down | Favor profitable value and quality; avoid chasing weak AI-credit names |
| Technology relief rally | Yields fall; semis turn broadly positive; CoreWeave holds its opening range | Cover tactical tech hedges; favor cash-rich leaders over leveraged operators |
| Inflation pressure returns | Import prices beat; production is firm; 10-year yield rises above 5.15% | Reduce duration exposure and leverage; use defined-risk hedges |
| Growth scare | Production misses sharply; banks weaken; yields fall with cyclicals | Favor liquidity and defensives; do not mistake lower yields for an automatic risk-on signal |
| Broad risk-off | Dow joins Nasdaq lower; credit stress spreads; breadth deteriorates | Prioritize capital preservation and wait for stabilization after the first hour |

Do not anchor to futures quoted before 8:30 AM. Import prices arrive before the open, industrial production lands at 9:15 AM, and bank results may shift sector leadership. Let the post-data range and the first 15–30 minutes of cash trading establish confirmation.

## 6. Bottom line

The pre-open tone is cautiously constructive for the broad market but still fragile beneath the indexes. Lower Treasury yields and a noncommittal Fed Chair are providing relief, while the Dow's weekly gain and Thursday's small-cap strength show that investors are rotating rather than abandoning equities. The unresolved problem is AI financing: demand remains strong, but markets are no longer willing to treat every debt-funded capacity plan as equally valuable.

The highest-quality read today will come from the interaction of the **10-year yield, Nasdaq-versus-Dow relative performance, regional-bank guidance, and AI-credit breadth**. A lower yield plus stabilizing semiconductors would support a relief rally. A post-data yield reversal with continued weakness in leveraged AI names would keep the week's correction active.
