## 2. What is driving the open

### Middle East escalation lifts oil

Several developments landed overnight:

- **Attacks in Saudi Arabia.** A drone strike on Saudi Aramco's Shaybah field killed two people but left operations unaffected, and an attack on King Fahd International Airport in Dammam killed one ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-21/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-oct-22)). Aramco told staff to evacuate its Dhahran headquarters and work remotely, and the U.S. ordered some diplomats and their families to leave the kingdom because of Houthi threats ([ZeroHedge](https://www.zerohedge.com/markets/futures-slide-market-runs-out-ai-patience-oil-jumps-saudi-attacks)).
- **Renewed strike plans.** The New York Times reported that the U.S. and Israel have drawn up plans for renewed strikes on Iran after the midterm elections, targeting energy infrastructure, nuclear facilities and missile and drone arsenals (as summarized by [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/stock-market-today-thursday-october-22-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-tesla-meta-112217857.html) and [Investrade](https://investrade.com/morning-preview-october-22-2026/)).
- **Strait of Hormuz.** Iran's IRGC Navy said four vessels transferring oil cargoes were struck in the Persian Gulf and three more hit sea mines west of the strait. An Iranian general said Iran will neither reopen the strait nor resume negotiations until seven new conditions are met ([ZeroHedge](https://www.zerohedge.com/markets/futures-slide-market-runs-out-ai-patience-oil-jumps-saudi-attacks)). These are Iranian claims and have not been independently confirmed here.

Brent is above Wednesday's $108.82 settle and the $110 line from this week's framework, and WTI briefly traded above $100. That revives inflation risk ahead of the October 28 FOMC decision.

### The AI trade unwinds

Wednesday's 1.97% semiconductor rally is reversing. Premarket losses were about 7% to 9% for Lam Research, about 5% for IBM, about 4% for Intel and AMD, about 3% for Broadcom and about 2% for Nvidia, Alphabet and Amazon ([ZeroHedge](https://www.zerohedge.com/markets/futures-slide-market-runs-out-ai-patience-oil-jumps-saudi-attacks), [Benzinga headline](https://www.benzinga.com/trading-ideas/movers/26/10/62522553/tesla-lam-research-rollins-and-other-big-stocks-moving-lower-in-thursdays-pre-market-session)). Lam fell despite beating and raising, and IBM gave up its initial after-hours gain, extending this week's "beat but sold" pattern.

**Tesla** is down about 3% to 5% premarket. It beat on adjusted EPS ($0.48) and revenue ($28.05 billion) but cut its 2026 capex target to about $20 billion from more than $25 billion while flagging heavier 2027–2028 spending on Optimus, Cybercab and Terafab ([Tesla's SEC-filed update](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1318605/000162828026066738/exhibit9914.htm), [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/stock-market-today-thursday-october-22-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-tesla-meta-112217857.html), [ZeroHedge](https://www.zerohedge.com/markets/futures-slide-market-runs-out-ai-patience-oil-jumps-saudi-attacks)). **Meta** has turned from a 2% to 4% after-hours gain to about -1.5%. One cached quote showed Tesla up about 3.4%, but that appears to be Wednesday's close and is not used here.

### Rates: reported Treasury rally

The 10-year yield is about 5.03% to 5.07%, down from 5.13%, and the 30-year touched its lowest since September 23 ([ZeroHedge](https://www.zerohedge.com/markets/futures-slide-market-runs-out-ai-patience-oil-jumps-saudi-attacks)). This is the reverse of earlier this week, when higher oil pushed yields up. Gold is down about 1.3% and silver more than 3%, so the haven demand is concentrated in Treasuries and the yen rather than metals.

The reported $26 billion 5-year TIPS auction at 1:00 PM ET tests demand for inflation-linked debt with oil above $110. The [Treasury schedule](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/221/TentativeAuctionScheduleQ22026.pdf) confirms the October 22 auction date; size and deadline remain secondary-source details here. Compare the auction's stop-out real yield with the when-issued real yield immediately before bidding closes, alongside bid-to-cover and bidder allocations. Strong demand alone does not establish higher inflation expectations; compare maturity-matched nominal and real yields to assess breakevens, which also reflect risk and liquidity premiums. The Fed is in its blackout period ahead of the October 28 decision.
