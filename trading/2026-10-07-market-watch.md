## 2. What caused the overnight / pre-market futures drop

**1) Rising Treasury yields (the dominant driver).** The 10-year UST yield reached its **highest since 2002 (~5.33–5.35%)** and the 30-year climbed above **5.7% (a 24-year high)**. Higher yields raise the discount rate on equities and press valuations — hardest on long-duration growth/tech. Critically, yields have **decoupled from oil**: the rise is now driven more by **fiscal deficits, heavy corporate borrowing, and soft foreign demand for U.S. debt**, not inflation alone.

**2) Oil / Middle East headline risk (live volatility engine).** Two competing narratives:
- *Supply relief:* increased Gulf exports + a European diesel release → WTI down ~2% toward the high-$80s, easing inflation fears.
- *Supply shock:* reports of Houthi/Hormuz tanker attacks and threats to Saudi oil facilities → Brent pushing back toward $100+.

Either direction can repivot the session within minutes.

**3) De-risking into the FOMC minutes.** Today at **2:00 PM ET** the Fed releases minutes from the **September FOMC meeting** (where it hiked 25 bp to a 3.75%–4.00% range). Market tension being priced:
- ~80% odds of a **hold** at the next meeting (CME FedWatch).
- ~65–75% odds of a **December hike** (per the September dot plot).
