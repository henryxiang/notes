# U.S. Market Watch — November 20, 2026

**Session:** Friday, November 20, 2026 · Pre-open analysis, compiled at about 8:15 to 8:20 AM ET from reports published through about 8:15 AM ET. Futures, oil and gold quotes carry CNBC's 8:05 to 8:07 AM ET timestamps (feed delayed). Yields, the VIX and the dollar index are from about 8:15 to 8:17 AM ET, and stock quotes from about 8:10 to 8:17 AM ET. There are no major market-moving 8:30 AM ET data releases today, although the BEA's annual U.S. multinational-enterprise statistics are due then; Governor Waller's 8:25 AM ET welcoming remarks were still ahead when this was written. ET is UTC-5. Section 7 adds a mid-session update, compiled at about 1:30 to 1:45 PM ET, and section 8 the after-close analysis, compiled at about 11:00 to 11:45 PM ET.

**Pre-open regime:** The AI selloff is deepening and spreading overseas. S&P 500 futures are down about 0.97%, Nasdaq-100 futures about 1.81%, Dow futures about 0.35% and Russell 2000 futures about 0.42%. That follows Thursday's 2.02% drop to 7,175.83, the S&P 500's sixth straight loss and its lowest close since April 29. Anthropic (ANT) is down about 9.5% near $87.95 in its second session, Nvidia about 3.2% near $141.36 after California's attorney general subpoenaed the company, and SMH about 3.2%. Intuit is down about 8.3% after cutting its revenue outlook. Taiwan's Taiex fell another 6.23% (a record 17.5% weekly loss), the Nikkei 5.06% and the Kospi 5.21%. Unlike Thursday, bonds are rallying: the 10-year yield is about 7 bp lower near 4.98% and the 2-year about 9 bp lower near 4.69%. The VIX is near 30.9, its highest since March. Gold is down about 2.9% near $3,580 after touching about $3,515.

**Pre-open summary:** Thursday's mid-session update said the morning's bearish case was playing out, and the close confirmed it: the S&P 500 finished within about 2 points of its low, every sector fell, and the Dow closed below 50,000 for the first time since June. Overnight, the selling moved to Asia and Europe, and the premarket shows two changes from Thursday. First, the selling is narrower: chips, AI infrastructure and software are down 3% to 6%, while equal weight (RSP) is about -0.1% and staples (XLP, about +1.3%), utilities and health care are higher. Second, Treasurys are now acting as a haven. Our interpretation: the market is shifting from a broad de-risking toward a more targeted exit from AI-linked stocks, with growth fears showing up in yields. Gold and bitcoin falling at the same time point to forced selling or deleveraging, not a calm rotation. The S&P 500 is indicated near 7,106 (rough guide), about 9.1% below its October 6 record close of 7,818.93 and about 1% above the 10% correction line (about 7,037). Monthly options expire today, which can exaggerate moves. The tests today are whether the S&P 500 holds the late-April lows (7,107.86 to 7,135.95), whether chips find buyers after the open for the first time this week, and whether ANT holds above its $83 offer price.

## 2. What is driving the open

### Anthropic: second-day selling

- **Premarket.** ANT is down about 9.5% near $87.95, after trading between about $87.33 and $91.98 premarket. It is about 6% above the $83 offer price, against 17% at Thursday's close and 34% at its opening trade ([Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/ANT/)). CNBC had it down 6% at 4:25 AM ET, so losses have widened ([CNBC live blog](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)).
- **Context.** TradingKey says investors are questioning AI valuations and infrastructure spending, and that the stock risks falling below its offer price ([TradingKey](https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/stocks/us-stocks/262315308-us-pre-market-anthropic-tradingkey)). No company-specific news was found this morning.
- **Our interpretation:** The offer price is now the key level. Underwriters often support new issues near the offer price in the first days of trading, but how much support there will be is not public. A break below $83 would be a visible sign that demand for AI equity is weakening. Disclosure: this note was prepared with the help of Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic; the coverage here is limited to reported facts and market data.

### Nvidia: a state subpoena

- **The subpoena.** California Attorney General Rob Bonta issued an investigative subpoena to Nvidia on Thursday, as part of a probe into whether its advanced chips or confidential information reached China and whether the company misled investors ([California attorney general](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-serves-investigative-subpoena-nvidia); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/nvidia-california-bonta-subpoena-china-chips.html)). The state announcement says the information will help determine whether Nvidia's actions violated California law; a subpoena is an investigative step, not a finding of wrongdoing. It follows the reported SEC civil probe and Jensen Huang's exit as CEO covered in the [November 18 note](2026-11-18-market-watch.md).
- **Premarket.** Nvidia is down about 3.2% near $141.36, after closing at $145.98 on Thursday. A close near this level would be the lowest since June 2025. TradingKey reports that Nvidia is in advanced talks to acquire Inception for at least $500 million.
- **Our interpretation:** The legal overhang is growing from one federal inquiry to a federal and a state one, and Focus Taiwan's coverage shows it is now weighing on the whole Asian supply chain. Nvidia's fundamentals (Tuesday's beat and raise) have not been the issue all week.

Debt-funded AI buildouts like SpaceX's are the kind of leverage that Barr and the Fed minutes have flagged, so that news may add to concerns rather than reassure.

### Software: Intuit cuts its outlook

- **Results.** Intuit's fiscal 2027 first-quarter revenue rose 12% to about $4.35 billion and adjusted EPS was $2.91, both above estimates. But it cut its full-year revenue outlook to $23.081 to $23.365 billion (8% to 9% growth), from $23.279 to $23.512 billion, because Credit Karma is slowing. It now expects Credit Karma revenue growth of 3% to 7%. Management cited higher-than-expected interest rates and a faster-than-anticipated shift by consumers toward AI-powered experiences. It kept its operating-income and EPS outlook ([Intuit results and guidance](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1331/intuit-reports-first-quarter-results-and-enters-agreement-to-sell-mailchimp-to-bending-spoons); [prepared remarks](https://investors.intuit.com/_assets/_ec6fa3d84099c818e3499aef167549b7/intuit/db/946/10425/webcast_transcript/Q1_FY27_Earnings_Script.pdf)).
- **Mailchimp.** Intuit agreed to sell Mailchimp to Bending Spoons for about $3.68 billion (about $3.31 billion in cash and $368 million in stock), expected to close in early 2027, subject to approvals and customary conditions ([Intuit 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000089687826000045/intu-20261031.htm)).
- **Reaction.** Intuit is down about 8.3% near $284, after a low of about $273 after hours. IGV is down about 2.5%, and Microsoft about 2.5%. Cybersecurity is giving back part of its gains: BUG is down about 2.6%, Palo Alto about 2.2% and CrowdStrike about 3.0%.
- **Bill Holdings** is down about 3.7% near $61.74 after agreeing to be acquired by TPG, with Corpay as a partner, for $63.25 a share in cash, about $6 billion (CNBC). The deal price is below Thursday's $64.08 close.
- **Our interpretation:** Intuit's explanation, that consumers are moving to AI tools faster than expected, is the "AI disrupts software" worry stated by a company. Together with the chip selloff, it means both sides of the AI trade (the builders and the businesses AI may displace) are under pressure at the same time.
