# U.S. Market Watch — November 23, 2026

**Session:** Monday, November 23, 2026 · Pre-open analysis, compiled at about 8:50 to 9:00 AM ET from reports published through about 8:55 AM ET. Futures, yields, commodities, the VIX and the dollar index are Yahoo quotes from about 8:38 to 8:45 AM ET, and stock quotes are from about 8:40 to 8:55 AM ET. The Chicago Fed National Activity Index was due at 8:30 AM ET, but its value could not be confirmed when this was written. This is a holiday-shortened week: markets are closed Thursday for Thanksgiving and close early on Friday. ET is UTC-5.

**Post-publication update:** Shortly after this pre-open snapshot was compiled, Nvidia announced that its board had voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, CEO and a director, effective immediately. Colette Kress remains CFO, and Harvey C. Jones remains board chair ([Nvidia](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-board-reinstates-jensen-huang-as-president-ceo-and-board-director)). The board said it received input from senior levels of the U.S. government and was confident the reinstatement posed no national-security risk; it also said the independent committee's investigation would continue to completion. This converts the morning's political catalyst into company action and supersedes the report's statements that Nvidia had not responded or that the bounce rested only on a headline. It does not resolve the SEC accounting focus, California subpoena or independent board investigation discussed below.

**Pre-open regime:** A sharp oversold rebound led by AI stocks. S&P 500 futures are up about 2.4%, Nasdaq-100 futures about 3.2%, Dow futures about 1.6% and Russell 2000 futures about 2.2%. That follows Friday's 3.70% drop to 6,910.39, the seventh straight loss, which put the S&P 500 and Dow in correction territory. Nvidia is up about 8.3% near $135.9 after President Trump posted on Sunday evening that he wants Nvidia's board to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO. SMH is up about 5.4%. CrowdStrike agreed to buy Zscaler in an all-stock deal valued at about $43.9 billion: Zscaler is up about 19% and CrowdStrike down about 8%. The VIX is down about 12.6% near 31.4, still above 30. Gold is up about 3% near $3,620, and the dollar index is above 104.

**Pre-open summary:** Friday's close set up a classic oversold bounce: a 7-day losing streak, an 8.6% weekly drop, a VIX close near 36 and the Nasdaq-100 about 49 points above the 20% bear-market line. The weekend gave buyers a reason: a political push to bring Huang back to Nvidia. The bounce is broad. RSP, IWM and every sector ETF we track except energy are higher, and gold and bitcoin are recovering too, which suggests Friday's forced selling has paused. Three things keep us cautious. First, futures peaked at about 5:00 AM ET and have faded about 0.6% since. Second, none of the investigations behind last week's Nvidia selling (the SEC accounting focus, California's subpoena, the board's own inquiry) has been resolved. Third, Treasurys are not rallying and the dollar is firm, so the rebound is not getting help from rates. The S&P 500 is indicated near 7,073 (rough guide), just above its 7,037 correction line. The tests today are whether the gap holds through the first hour, whether the S&P 500 closes back above 7,037.04, and whether Nvidia keeps most of its gain.

## 2. What is driving the open

### Nvidia: a push to bring Huang back, followed by reinstatement

**Update:** After this snapshot was compiled, Nvidia reinstated Huang as president, CEO and a director. See the post-publication update above. The bullets below describe the information available before that announcement.

- **The post.** President Trump posted on Truth Social on Sunday evening urging Nvidia's board to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO; Investing.com paraphrases him as saying this would be best for Nvidia and the United States ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/why-is-nvidia-stock-surging-today-93CH-5045184)). We did not see the post's exact text. A CNBC live blog also attributes Nvidia's premarket gain to the post ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/22/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)).
- **Bessent.** Reports on Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent differ. Bloomingbit says he publicly called over the weekend for Huang to return ([Bloomingbit](https://en.bloomingbit.io/feed/news/125046)). Investing.com says he softened his earlier view that Nvidia needed an outside CEO. We could not find his exact words. After Friday's close, he told CNBC that no additional restrictions on older-chip sales to China are needed (see the November 20 note).
- **Background.** Huang resigned as president, CEO and director on November 12, and CFO Colette Kress was appointed interim chief executive ([Nvidia](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-ceo-transition)). Bloomingbit reports that Polymarket's odds of Huang returning as CEO rose to 19% from 11%. As of the snapshot's cutoff, none of the sources checked reported a new statement from Nvidia.
- **Premarket.** Nvidia is up about 8.3% near $135.88, after trading between about $133.85 and $137.93 this morning ([Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/NVDA/)). That is still below Thursday's $145.98 close.
- **Still open.** Nvidia has not yet filed its quarterly 10-Q, but it is not late: as a large accelerated filer, it has 40 days after its October 25 quarter-end, making December 4 the regular deadline ([SEC Form 10-Q instructions](https://www.sec.gov/about/forms/form10-q.pdf); [Nvidia's latest filings](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/browse/?CIK=1045810&owner=exclude)). Nvidia has not publicly attributed the timing to the investigations. Reuters' Friday report that accounting is a focus of the SEC investigation, California's subpoena, and the board's independent investigation led by Joon H. Kim of Wachtell Lipton ([Nvidia board statement](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/Statement-from-the-Independent-Members-of-the-NVIDIA-Board/default.aspx)) all remain unresolved.
- **Our interpretation:** Huang's reinstatement addresses the immediate leadership uncertainty and gives the rally a company-confirmed catalyst. It does not answer the accounting and export-control questions that drove Friday's 14% drop, so any new report on the SEC probe, California subpoena or 10-Q remains a material risk.

### CrowdStrike to buy Zscaler

- **The deal.** CrowdStrike will acquire Zscaler in an all-stock deal: 1.135 CrowdStrike shares per Zscaler share, about $260.41 per share and about $43.9 billion in enterprise value based on November 20 closes, a premium of about 34%. CrowdStrike holders would own about 84.1% of the combined company. The deal needs Zscaler shareholder and regulatory approvals; Jay Chaudhry and affiliates, with about 34% of Zscaler's shares, have signed voting agreements. Closing is expected in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal 2028 (roughly August 2027 to January 2028). CrowdStrike targets about $500 million in annual cost synergies within 18 to 24 months of closing ([CrowdStrike 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001535527/000110465926130785/tm2630454d1_ex99-1.htm)).
- **CrowdStrike's results.** CrowdStrike also moved up its earnings. Third-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue rose 28% to about $1.58 billion, ARR rose 27% to $6.26 billion, and net new ARR was a record $419.2 million. Non-GAAP EPS was $0.34. Its fourth-quarter guide is revenue of $1,638.1 to $1,644.1 million and non-GAAP EPS of $0.36 ([CrowdStrike results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001535527/000153552726000035/crwd-20261123xex991.htm)). We could not compare this with analysts' estimates.
- **Reaction.** Zscaler is up about 19.4% near $232.56, and CrowdStrike down about 7.9% near $211.28. At these prices the deal values Zscaler at about $239.80, about 3% above its premarket price (our calculation). BUG is up about 4.0%, and Palo Alto about 1.3%.
- **Our interpretation:** Bloomberg and Reuters had reported the talks after Friday's close, so the deal is not a surprise. Paying in stock after a week in which CrowdStrike fell sharply means Zscaler holders take on CrowdStrike's share-price risk until a closing that may be more than a year away.

### UnitedHealth and managed care: a new CMS proposal

- **The proposal.** CMS confirms that its November 20 proposed rule would narrow the Medicare Advantage and Part D Star Ratings program to 15 measures and change the calculation methodology to raise the bar for high ratings and associated quality bonus payments. The measure removals would apply to the 2028 measurement period and 2030 Star Ratings ([CMS fact sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/contract-year-2028-policy-technical-changes-medicare-advantage-program-medicare-prescription-drug)). Investing.com cites TD Cowen's Ryan Langston as estimating about $107 billion in lower Medicare Advantage payments over 2028–2037 based on CMS projections ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/why-is-unitedhealth-stock-sliding-today-93CH-5045559)); we did not independently reproduce that estimate.
- **Premarket.** UnitedHealth is down about 5.8% near $320, after a low of about $315. Elevance is down about 3.6%, CVS about 3.4% and Humana about 3.3%; Cigna, which has less Medicare Advantage exposure, is about flat ([Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/UNH/)).
- **Our interpretation:** This follows last week's CMS audit proposal, which sent UnitedHealth down 10% on November 18. Managed care is falling on regulation, not on last week's AI selling, so it is unlikely to join a rebound.
