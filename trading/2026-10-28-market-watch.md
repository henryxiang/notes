## 2. What is driving the open

### The Fed: a hawkish hold is the base case

The FOMC statement is due at 2:00 PM ET, followed by Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference at 2:30 PM ET ([Federal Reserve calendar](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/2026-october.htm)). The target range is 3.75% to 4.00% after the September 16 quarter-point hike ([Fed statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm)). CME FedWatch priced a 90.6% chance of a hold and an 84.3% chance of a December hike ([FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/news/dow-jones-futures-decline-ahead-of-fed-decision-big-tech-earnings-202610280856)). Yahoo puts hold odds at about 92%. LSEG data show traders pricing at least three 25 bp hikes by late 2027 ([Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/nasdaq-futures-rise-as-sk-hynix-boosts-chips-before-fed-decision-earnings-4978026)). Tuesday's prediction-market odds for a December hike were lower, at about 72.5% to 74.5% ([Capp Notes](https://cappnotes.substack.com/p/closing-look-tuesday-october-27-2026)); that is a different instrument.

Alan Blinder expects a "hawkish pause," with guidance hinting at a possible future hike ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-wednesday-october-28-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-fed-meeting-decision-080642111.html)). With December already largely priced, the statement's language on further firming and Warsh's tone matter more than the decision itself. Tuesday's data were mixed for the Fed: consumer confidence jumped and inflation expectations eased to 5.8%, but Dallas services wage and selling-price pressures rose (see the [October 27 note](2026-10-27-market-watch.md)).

The 2-year yield has risen for two straight days, while the 10-year is pinned at 5.00%. Tuesday's 5-year auction cleared at 4.845% with a 0.4 bp tail; the 5-year is now near 4.864%, a bit above that level.

Investing.com's consensus is EPS of $4.72 on revenue of $90.61 billion for Microsoft and EPS of $3.02 on $127.69 billion for Alphabet ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/alphabet-vs-microsoft-which-stock-has-the-better-setup-ahead-of-earnings-93CH-4975561), published October 27). - **Microsoft** is down about 0.2% near a record high. Eligibility does not establish how many will leave.
- **Meta** closed Tuesday at $620.19, below its $625 offering price, ahead of the offering's expected October 29 closing. FXStreet reports the $25 billion common-stock offering was five times oversubscribed; this was not verified against a company source.

### Memory leads; the after-hours chip losers recover partly

SK Hynix’s [company release](https://news.skhynix.com/en/q3-2026-business-results/) reports revenue of about 104.62 trillion won (+328% year over year) and operating profit of about 83.33 trillion won (+632%), with an operating margin of about 80%. The company expects memory shortages through 2030 and says long-term agreements cover more than 50% of total 2027–2028 production capacity. That does not substantiate the earlier secondary claim that DRAM and HBM capacity is sold out through 2029.

The release targets about 100 trillion won of total shareholder returns for 2026. Today’s board decisions comprise a 14 trillion won dividend and a 20 trillion won buyback; about half the buyback is for employee compensation and excluded from shareholder-return totals. These are different scopes, so the earlier “more than $23 billion” headline should not be read as the full-year return plan. Figures here remain in won to avoid an unstated exchange-rate assumption.

Memory peers are following: Micron about +2% to +4%, Western Digital about +4.5%, AMD about +1.5%, Marvell about +1.7% and Nvidia about +0.8% ([Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/nasdaq-futures-rise-as-sk-hynix-boosts-chips-before-fed-decision-earnings-4978026)). Seagate is down about 3% to 3.7%, narrower than its roughly 6% to 8% after-hours decline. Investrade says NXP and Teradyne are also off their overnight lows. Whether those losses fade is the first test of the three-day chip rally.

### Oil: Houthi attacks and a post-midterm strike warning

Oil is reversing most of Tuesday's drop. The Wall Street Journal reported that Washington warned several Gulf Arab countries to prepare for a possible renewed U.S. military campaign against Iran after the November 3 midterms ([The National](https://thenationalnews.com/business/energy/2026/10/28/oil-prices-up-due-to-houthi-strikes-and-us-threat-to-renew-iran-war)). A possible campaign after the election is compatible with Tuesday’s secondary report of no attack before the election. Neither report rules out later action; the warning remains attributed reporting rather than a confirmed strike decision. President Trump said Sunday he had a "decision to make" on Iran.

Attacks on Saudi Arabia continue. The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted a Houthi ballistic missile aimed at Taif on Wednesday morning, and the Houthis claimed a drone strike on Abha airport, which Saudi officials had not confirmed. StockTwits ties the oil gain to Houthi reports of an attack on Ras Tanura; no confirmed lasting damage has been reported.

Diplomacy continues. Qatari mediators presented Iran with a new draft deal, and an official told Reuters that Tehran is expected to respond by the end of the week. UBS raised its forecasts and expects Brent near $110 through year-end, citing tight supply, falling inventories and record freight costs ([The National](https://thenationalnews.com/business/energy/2026/10/28/oil-prices-up-due-to-houthi-strikes-and-us-threat-to-renew-iran-war)).
