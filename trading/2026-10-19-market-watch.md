## 2. What mattered

### Technology bounced, but the rally was narrow

AI and technology shares recovered some of their steep losses from the prior week, lifting the Nasdaq by 1.1%. The rest of the market was less convincing: the Dow slipped, small caps posted only a modest gain, and the Associated Press reported that most U.S. stocks outside AI and technology declined.

That combination supports a **relief-rally** interpretation. A stronger trend-change signal would require broader participation from cyclicals, financials, small caps, and the equal-weight market.

### Rates moved against the growth-stock rally

The official 10-year Treasury constant-maturity yield finished at 5.15%, six basis points above Friday. That removes the lower-yield support assumed in the morning thesis and leaves highly valued growth stocks vulnerable to another duration-driven reversal.

The next test for the long end is Wednesday's $13 billion 20-year Treasury reopening. Thursday's $26 billion 5-year TIPS auction will provide a separate read on real-yield and inflation demand. The auction sizes come from the [Treasury's August quarterly refunding statement](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0590), and the dates appear in its [tentative auction schedule](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/221/Tentative-Auction-Schedule.pdf).

### Housing sentiment weakened further

The October NAHB/Wells Fargo Housing Market Index fell three points to 29, its lowest reading since June 2012. All three components declined, while 43% of builders reported cutting prices and 65% reported using sales incentives. NAHB attributed the weakness to rising mortgage rates, energy and material costs, and labor and lot constraints. ([NAHB](https://www.nahb.org/news-and-economics/press-releases/2026/10/builder-sentiment-falls-on-rising-rates))

The result reinforces the message from the bond market: rates above 5% are exerting pressure beyond technology, particularly on housing and other financing-sensitive areas.

## 5. Framework for Tuesday

### Bullish confirmation

- Participation broadens to the Dow, small caps, financials, and other cyclical groups.
- Housing data do not materially worsen the growth-versus-inflation tradeoff.

- Monday's technology gains reverse while the 10-year yield remains at or above 5.15%.
- The equal-weight market and small caps continue to lag.
- Weak housing data deepen growth concerns without producing a meaningful decline in yields.

The most useful signal is no longer whether technology can bounce for one session. It is whether technology can retain those gains while rates are elevated and market breadth improves.
