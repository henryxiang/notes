## 2. What is driving the open

### Apple's margin guide drags the Dow

Apple is down about 4% premarket. CNBC cites its "disappointing margin forecast" and fiscal Q4 iPad sales below estimates ([CNBC premarket movers](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/03/stocks-making-the-biggest-moves-premarket-pfe-pltr-aapl-zbra.html), 7:47 AM ET). Yahoo's ticker dock shows $324.68 (-$13.64, -4.03%) against Monday's $338.32 close, below Monday's $335.84 low and the roughly $329 overnight quote cited Monday night.

- **What drove it.** Apple's December-quarter gross-margin guide is 45% to 46%, against 47.9% reported. About 1 point of the reported margin came from tariff refunds. Management attributed the expected decline primarily to higher memory costs and expenses for Siri AI ([Apple's earnings release](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/11/apple-reports-fourth-quarter-results/); [AP](https://apnews.com/article/9698be9e0226091a537611382144eb44)).
- **Dow mechanics.** Apple is a price-weighted Dow component, which helps explain why Dow futures lag the S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100. Apple alone does not account for the whole Dow futures decline; no constituent breakdown was checked.

### Palantir falls despite a beat

Palantir is down about 6% to 8% premarket (CNBC "nearly 8%," 7:47 AM ET; Yahoo dock $167.70, -5.97%). Revenue of $2.313 billion beat its own guide and U.S. commercial revenue rose 148%, but the AP attributes the drop to a slowdown in U.S. commercial bookings. That bookings figure was not checked against the company's release. Our interpretation: with results this far ahead of the guide, the reaction looks more like a test of the stock's valuation than a response to a miss.

### Chips split: Asia sells, U.S. names bounce premarket

- **Asia.** The Kospi fell 3.59% and Taiwan reportedly 3.59%, led by SK Hynix (about -5.7%) and Samsung (about -4%). [TradingKey](https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/stocks/more/262265677-japan-korea-stocks-skhy-kospi-nikkei-samsung-mrvl-orcl-ai-tradingkey) and [BigGo](https://finance.biggo.com/news/a59ec2f2-706f-4843-81eb-94362411dd24) link the drop to Monday's U.S. chip selloff (Marvell, Oracle) and renewed doubts about AI spending; the AP cites safe-haven demand before the midterms.
- **Europe.** European chip stocks fell at the open: ASML -3.4%, ASM International -2.1%, STMicroelectronics and Infineon -3.9% (CNBC).
- **U.S. premarket.** CNBC reports Micron up almost 4%, AMD and Intel up more than 3%, and Broadcom and Nvidia up about 2%. Monday's U.S. bounce also started this way: Micron was up about 2% premarket and closed down 5.78%.
- **Our interpretation:** Asian memory makers falling while Micron rises is an unusual split. Apple's memory-cost warning cuts two ways: it points to strong memory pricing, but also to pressure on device makers' margins. A premarket bounce after two down sessions has already failed once this week; whether it lasts after 10:00 AM ET is the key chip test.

### Oil rebounds after reported U.S. strikes on Qeshm Island

- **The strikes.** The Wall Street Journal, citing U.S. officials, reported U.S. strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island, at the mouth of the Strait of Hormuz, on Monday night. Officials described them as defensive, meant to stop Iran attacking commercial shipping. Iranian state media reported several explosions ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/energy/2026/11/03/oil-prices-cross-105-after-us-strikes-on-irans-qeshm-island/); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/03/oil-rises-after-report-of-us-strike-on-irans-qeshm-island.html)). U.S. Central Command had not publicly confirmed the strikes in the reports reviewed.
- **Limited strikes, not the major campaign.** The WSJ described this as the latest in a series of limited U.S. strikes in recent weeks, some kept secret to avoid signaling a resumption of the major campaign (The National). Saxo's summary says Iran's leadership authorized further talks with Washington and that President Trump reportedly said strikes would not resume after the midterms. These statements have not been checked against primary sources.
- **Price action.** Brent rose about 2% to nearly $107 and WTI to about $98 in Asian and early European trading. By 8:10 AM ET, WTI had eased to about $97 (+1%) and Brent to about $105 (CNBC). Emirates NBD's Daniel Richards said the strikes "renewed uncertainty" for the diplomatic opening (The National).
- **Our interpretation:** Oil has recovered only a fraction of Monday's 7%-plus drop, so markets still seem to price diplomacy as the more likely path. Another strike or a ship attack would test that.

### Rates edge up; RBA hikes but signals a possible peak

- **Treasuries.** The 10-year is about 4.91%, up 1 to 2 bp from Monday's 4.894% ^TNX close, and the 2-year about 4.59% to 4.60% (CNBC; Saxo). U.S. high-yield spreads are about 339 bp, 1 bp below their 18-month widest (Saxo).
- **Fed odds.** No same-day CME FedWatch reading was found. Monday's cited range was 72% to 76% for a December hike ([CME FedWatch](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/interest-rates/cme-fedwatch-tool.html) not checked directly). The next FOMC decision is December 9.
- **Australia.** The RBA raised its cash rate 25 bp to 4.85%, an 18-year high and its fifth hike of 2026, on a 6–3 vote. It dropped its reference to further increases if needed, and Governor Michele Bullock said the next move was unclear. The Australian dollar fell about 0.3% to about $0.698 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/03/australia-hikes-rates-18-year-high-finflation.html); [RTÉ](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2026/1103/1597738-australia-lifts-rates-to-18-year-high/); [BusinessWorld](https://bworldonline.com/banking-finance/2026/11/03/791079/rba-lifts-rates-to-18-year-high/)). Our interpretation: a split vote and softer wording at a hiking central bank is a modest signal that this year's global tightening may be near its peak; it does not directly change the Fed's December decision.

### Midterm elections

- **Odds.** Prediction markets give Democrats about a 73.1% chance of Senate control, against 80.3% in polling-based models; the gap was 7.2 points on Monday (Yahoo). CNBC cites prediction-market traders at nearly 75%. Both sources favor Democrats to take the House; no probability was found. The underlying markets and models were not checked.
- **Timing (ET).** Polls first close in parts of Indiana and Kentucky at 6:00 PM ET. The first broad wave is at 7:00 PM ET, including Georgia, South Carolina and Virginia, followed at 8:00 PM ET by Maine, Michigan, Pennsylvania and most of Texas ([AP election-night agenda](https://apnews.com/article/0e2078f16af285b22675445d78360e6d)). Results will come after the cash close; states spanning time zones can have later final closures.
- **Market framing.** CNBC notes a Democratic sweep could weigh on the broader market and AI stocks, while Republican control could bring short-term gains. Carson Group's Ryan Detrick: "Once you are through it, it's a big relief and markets tend to do pretty well." Yahoo highlights ballot measures on drug prices, housing and AI data centers.
- **Our interpretation:** Options price about a 0.86% S&P 500 move today and 1.85% through Friday (Saxo). That points to positioning for the overnight result and Friday's jobs report, not a large move during today's session.
