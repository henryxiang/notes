**Pre-open regime:** Monday's rally is fading, led by AI stocks. S&P 500 futures are down about 1.3%, Nasdaq-100 futures about 2.1%, Dow futures about 0.7% and Russell 2000 futures about 0.9%. Nvidia is down about 5.6% near $155.9, after Monday's 31.5% gain on Jensen Huang's reinstatement. Political criticism of how he was reinstated has widened to include some Republican lawmakers. SMH is down about 5.2%. Analog Devices is down about 10.8% after its results. Anthropic (ANT) is down about 5.3% near $82.97 and traded below its $83 offer price for the first time. The VIX is up about 13.7% near 30.3, back above 30. Gains are in deal and earnings stocks: Insmed is up about 14.5% on a $41 billion cash offer from Sanofi, and Dick's, Kohl's, Dollar Tree, Burlington and Best Buy are higher after their results.

**Pre-open summary:** Monday was the S&P 500's best day since April 2025, and one stock made an outsized contribution: CNBC's Michael Santoli estimates that about a third of the index's gain came from Nvidia. Today's question is how much of that gain holds. The S&P 500 is indicated near 7,147 (rough guide), below Monday's 7,152.66 intraday low and Thursday's 7,175.83 close. That would give back about 28% of Monday's 327-point gain, and leave the index about 110 points above its 7,037.04 correction line. Futures have fallen steadily since about 2:00 AM ET, from about 7,284 to about 7,160, without a clear new headline. That pattern fits profit-taking more than a fresh shock. Three things stand out. First, the selling is concentrated in the stocks that rose most on Monday, while health care, staples, utilities and energy ETFs are slightly higher. Second, ANT's break of $83 removes the IPO-price support that the last two notes had treated as a floor. Third, Treasurys and gold are not rallying, so this looks like a rotation out of AI rather than a broad move away from risk. The tests today are whether the S&P 500 holds 7,152.66 and then 7,037.04, whether Nvidia holds above about $146 (Thursday's close), and whether ANT gets back above $83.

## 2. What is driving the open

### Nvidia: profit-taking and political criticism of the reinstatement

- **Premarket.** Nvidia is down about 5.6% near $155.87, after trading between about $154.85 and $164.40 this morning ([Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/NVDA/)). That is still about 7% above Thursday's $145.98 close, and keeps most of Monday's 31.5% gain. CNBC had it down 5.1% at 7:41 AM ET ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/24/nvidia-stock-jensen-huang-return.html)).
- **Trump's role.** President Trump told CNBC on Monday that "I made the deal," and said Nvidia's board had asked for assurances that Huang was not a national-security threat and that "we gave them assurances" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/24/nvidia-stock-jensen-huang-return.html)).
- **Criticism from both parties.** Sen. Elizabeth Warren, expected to chair the Senate Banking Committee in January, called the reinstatement "reckless" and said Nvidia still owes Congress answers on export-control compliance (CNBC). Republican Sen. Pete Ricketts said Huang's "behavior does not support reinstatement." ABC reports that Sens. Kevin Cramer, John Curtis and Tom Cotton also raised concerns, and that Reps. Rick Crawford, Brian Mast and John Moolenaar said the company's "brief internal investigation" does not "erase the national security threats" ([ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Politics/republicans-condemn-jensen-huangs-reinstatement-nvidia-after-trump/story?id=138384161)). New York City Comptroller Mark Levine, whose pension funds hold about 5 million Nvidia shares, told Reuters the board has "proven they're not independent" (per the [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/business/live/2026/nov/24/jensen-huang-nvidia-stock-markets-ai-trade-us-political-backlash-donald-trump-latest-news-updates)).
- **Hearings next week.** Huang is due to sit for a closed-door interview with three House committees on Tuesday, December 1, and is expected to testify at a Senate Banking Committee hearing on Thursday, December 3, at 10:00 AM ET. The hearing is titled "Protecting America's AI Advantage: Oversight of U.S. Export Controls" ([ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Politics/republicans-condemn-jensen-huangs-reinstatement-nvidia-after-trump/story?id=138384161); [Senate Banking Committee](https://www.banking.senate.gov/hearings/protecting-americas-ai-advantage-oversight-of-us-export-controls)). The committee page confirms the hearing's date and time; ABC is the source for Huang's expected appearance.
- **Megaspeed.** The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security is adding seven entities in Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand to its Entity List, including three Megaspeed entities. BIS cites a significant risk of diversion of advanced-computing products to unauthorized end uses or end users. The rule requires a license for all items subject to the Export Administration Regulations and applies a presumption of denial; publication is scheduled for Wednesday ([Federal Register public inspection](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-23999.pdf)).
- **Analysts.** Bank of America wrote that the reinstatement "should not be confused with the end of scrutiny," pointing to governance, China and export controls, customer relationships and compliance (CNBC). Monday's options volume in Nvidia exceeded 11 million contracts, about 6.7 million of them calls, per Cboe LiveVol data cited by CNBC.
- **Still open.** We found no new reports this morning on the SEC's accounting focus, California's subpoena or Nvidia's third-quarter 10-Q, which is due by Friday, December 4 (see the November 23 note).
- **Our interpretation:** A pullback after a 31% day would be normal on its own. What is new is that the criticism has become bipartisan and is now tied to hearing dates. Next week's hearings and the December 4 10-Q deadline are the next scheduled tests. Holding above Thursday's $145.98 close would show that buyers still accept Monday's re-rating.

### Sanofi to buy Insmed

- **The deal.** Sanofi agreed to buy Insmed for $175 per share in cash, representing about $41 billion in equity value; CNBC describes it as the biggest pharmaceutical deal since 2023. Both boards approved the transaction, which requires Insmed shareholder approval, U.S. and certain non-U.S. regulatory approvals and other customary conditions. Sanofi expects it to close in the first half of 2027 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/24/stocks-moving-the-most-today-live-updates.html); [Sanofi press release](https://www.sanofi.com/en/media-room/press-releases/2026/2026-11-24-06-30-00-3409002)).
- **Reaction.** Insmed is up about 14.5% near $170.70, about 2.5% below the offer price; the offer is about 17% above Monday's $149.09 close (our calculations). Sanofi's U.S. shares are up about 6.7% ([Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/SNY/)).
- **Our interpretation:** The 2.5% gap to the offer price is small for a deal of this size, which suggests the market expects few regulatory problems. Large biotech takeovers, along with Monday's CrowdStrike–Zscaler deal, show that companies are still doing big deals despite last week's selloff.

- **Kohl's.** Third-quarter revenue of $3.5 billion compares with $3.39 billion expected (LSEG), and the operating margin was 4.3% versus about 2% expected (FactSet). Kohl's raised its full-year guidance (CNBC). Up about 7.9%.

Burlington's soft comparable sales and the one-time tax, real-estate and tariff benefits in Kohl's results argue for caution. Consumer confidence at 10:00 AM ET and Wednesday's spending data provide broader tests.
